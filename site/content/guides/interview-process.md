Some employers use standardized cohort hiring, while others adapt interviews by role and team. Knowing the stages lets you prepare for the right one at the right time instead of everything at once. Knowing that each stage can take several formats — human or AI, live or asynchronous, with or without permitted AI tooling — lets you match your preparation to the actual evaluation, not the one you wish the employer had given you.

## The stages

1. **Application review.** A recruiter or a rule screens the résumé against the posting. Days to weeks. [An ATS-friendly résumé](../ats-friendly-resume/) and [Tailoring your résumé](../tailoring-your-resume/) are about this stage. If the funnel is silent past a hundred applications, see [No interviews after 100 applications](../no-interviews-after-100-applications/).
2. **Assessment or screen.** One of: a coding assessment, an AI-scored recorded interview, a short live recruiter call, a work sample, an asynchronous video response, a chat with a chatbot, or a take-home. Sometimes sent to everyone before a human has read the résumé. The rules — live or async, AI allowed or prohibited, recording, time limits — are spelled out in the invitation; read them. See [AI-assisted interviews](../ai-assisted-interviews/) and [Coding assessments](../coding-assessments/).
3. **Technical evaluation.** The format can be: human-led coding interview, AI-mediated coding session, take-home with permitted AI, asynchronous coding challenge with no AI, work sample review, pair-programming exercise, system design conversation, or a domain-specific case (data, ML, security, embedded). See [Coding interviews](../coding-interviews/), [How to handle an interview question you don't know](../how-to-handle-an-interview-question-you-dont-know/), and [System design](../system-design/).
4. **Final loop.** Several conversations in one day or across a week: more technical rounds, a [behavioral interview](../behavioral-interviews/), often a hiring-manager chat, sometimes a values or bar-raiser round. See [How to prepare for a multi-round interview loop](../how-to-prepare-for-a-multi-round-interview-loop/) for how to pace across the day.
5. **Decision and offer.** Days to weeks. Then the process in the [Offers](../evaluating-an-offer/) guides begins. For the last week before the loop, see [How to prepare for an interview in 7 days](../how-to-prepare-for-an-interview-in-7-days/).

## The format matrix

Stages may combine the formats below. The same role at two different employers may use different combinations, and the rules change with each platform and each employer.

- **Human-led** — the traditional format. A person is on the other end, real-time.
- **AI-mediated** — a chatbot or voice assistant runs the conversation. Follow-ups may be adaptive.
- **AI-assisted** — a human runs the loop but an AI tool is permitted or expected on the candidate side.
- **Automated scoring** — software evaluates tests or responses; this does not tell you whether a human reviews the result or makes the hiring decision.
- **Asynchronous** — you complete the round on your own time within a window, with no live counterpart.
- **Hybrid** — a mix within the same round (e.g., a human interviewer plus an AI coding assistant in a shared editor).
- **Take-home** — an open-ended deliverable, usually with a stated time budget.
- **Live coding** — a coding problem solved in a shared editor, with or without AI tooling allowed.
- **Work sample** — a small piece of work resembling the job, reviewed after submission.

Don't optimize for the interview format you wish companies used. Optimize for the evaluation format the company actually gave you. See [AI-assisted interviews](../ai-assisted-interviews/) for the workflow that gets you there.

## Timelines

The whole loop takes anywhere from two weeks to three months. Cohort programs at large employers are the slowest and the most parallel: they interview many candidates in the same window and make decisions in batches. If a recruiter gives you a date, believe it; if they do not, ask for one at every stage.

## What is judged, by stage

- Application: relevance and clarity.
- Assessment or screen: eligibility, communication, and a baseline skill signal — the exact skill depends on the format.
- Technical evaluation: problem solving under the format's constraints (live with a person, take-home with permitted AI, AI-mediated with adaptive follow-ups).
- Final loop: depth, collaboration, judgment, motivation, and consistency across interviewers.

Notice that talking clearly is judged at every stage after the first, including AI-scored recorded rounds. Practicing out loud is not optional.

## Managing several loops at once

Interviews cluster. When you have an offer from one company and are mid-loop at another, tell the second recruiter; most will accelerate. When you have two offers, [Comparing offers](../comparing-offers/) applies. Keep every date, every platform, and every format note in your tracker. The format a company uses is part of the data you keep.

## After a rejection

Ask for feedback; a minority of employers give it, and it is worth the one-line email. Note which stage and which format you reached. A rejection stage identifies where to investigate; it does not prove why you were rejected. Consider role fit, eligibility, demonstrated skills, and employer-side changes. Reapply only when the employer's waiting period and eligibility rules permit it.
