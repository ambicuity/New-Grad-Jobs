Once an offer is on the table you have more leverage to ask questions than at any other point, and the answers are the last information you will get before you commit a year or more. Ask them. Employers expect it, and a hesitant answer is itself information.

## About the work

1. What will I work on in the first three months, specifically?
2. Who decides that, and how is work assigned on the team?
3. What does a good first year look like, and how will I know?
4. What is the team's main project this year, and how central is it to the company?
5. How much of the job is the technology or skill in the posting, versus other things?

## About the team and manager

6. Who will my manager be? (If the answer is "to be decided", ask when, and by whom.)
7. How many people are on the team, and how many are in their first or second year?
8. How are new graduates onboarded: a program, a buddy, or learn as you go?
9. How often will I get feedback, and in what form?
10. Who left the team in the last year, and why? (Ask the manager; a good one answers.)

## About growth and pay

11. When are performance reviews and raises, and is a first-year hire included?
12. What is the typical time to the next level for someone starting here?
13. How was the bonus paid against target last year?
14. For equity: units, vesting schedule, most recent valuation, and for options the exercise window after leaving. See [Understanding compensation](../understanding-compensation/).
15. Is there a learning budget, conference support or a rotation program?

## About logistics

16. What is the remote or hybrid policy for this role, and is it a policy or a manager's preference?
17. Is the start date flexible?
18. What does relocation cover, and when is it paid?
19. For international candidates: are you enrolled in E-Verify, and what is your policy on future sponsorship? See [OPT and STEM OPT](../opt-and-stem-opt/).
20. For startups: how much runway do you have, and what happens to this role if the next round does not close?

## How to ask

Send the questions to the recruiter and ask for a short call with the hiring manager for the ones about the work and the team. Most managers welcome it; a manager who will not spend thirty minutes with a candidate who holds an offer is a data point. Take notes and put them in your tracker next to the offer.

## What the answers tell you

Specific answers mean the team has thought about you; vague ones mean it has not, and the first months will be spent working that out on the job. Consistent answers from recruiter and manager are reassuring; contradictions are worth a follow-up. Then decide with [Evaluating an offer](../evaluating-an-offer/) or [Comparing offers](../comparing-offers/).
