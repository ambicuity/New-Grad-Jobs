There is no single market for every new graduate. Country, occupation, location, work authorization and employer budgets affect which openings you can pursue. This snapshot uses sources checked on September 30, 2026. The periods below are the periods measured, not a forecast for the rest of the year.

## United States: hiring plans and graduate outcomes differ

The New York Fed reports about 5.6% unemployment and 42% underemployment for recent college graduates in the second quarter of 2026. Its recent-graduate group is ages 22–27 with a bachelor's degree or higher; underemployment means working in an occupation that typically does not require a degree. It does not mean every such job is low paid or temporary. See the [New York Fed's graduate labor-market data and definitions](https://www.newyorkfed.org/research/college-labor-market).

NACE's April 2026 Spring Update projects 5.6% more hiring for the Class of 2026 than the previous class, with uneven changes by industry and employer type. This is an employer survey of hiring intentions, not a count of completed hires or your chance of receiving an offer. It also reports a shift toward spring recruitment, so missing the autumn cycle does not mean every opportunity has gone. See [NACE's Job Outlook 2026 Spring Update](https://www.naceweb.org/research/reports/2026/job-outlook/spring-update/).

These measures can coexist: some surveyed employers plan to expand while graduates still face a difficult search. Neither justifies a universal application response-rate target or a claim that a particular field is guaranteed to hire.

## Canada: youth data are broader than graduate data

Statistics Canada reports a 12.9% unemployment rate for ages 15–24 in August 2026, above its 2017–2019 average. That group includes students and people without a degree and excludes graduates aged 25 and over. It is not directly comparable to the US graduate measure. Read the [August 2026 Labour Force Survey](https://www150.statcan.gc.ca/n1/daily-quotidien/260904/dq260904a-eng.htm), then check opportunities in your province and occupation rather than using the national rate as a personal prediction.

## India: national employment data are not campus placement data

MoSPI's August 2026 PLFS release reports 5.0% unemployment for people aged 15 and above under its Current Weekly Status measure. This covers a broad population and a short reference period; it does not measure the placement rate of new engineering, business or other graduates. See the [official August 2026 PLFS press note](https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleaseIframePage.aspx?PRID=2310427&lang=2&reg=48).

Check each employer's graduation-batch eligibility, degree requirements, campus or off-campus route, location and joining date. Ask your placement office about rules that may limit participation after accepting an offer. A national headline cannot establish whether your batch or college is eligible for a particular drive.

## How to use this evidence

The following is practical search advice, not a measured guarantee of success:

- Build a target list across relevant employers and role families. Use actual job requirements to decide fit, rather than a claim that an entire sector is booming or dead.
- Track comparable applications by role, country, authorization and submission date. Compare your own results over time; do not judge yourself against an invented response-rate benchmark.
- Show evidence of skills through work, coursework, projects, placements or clinical experience appropriate to your field. Include outcomes you can explain and verify.
- Prepare for the employer's stated assessment format. AI skills in a job description do not grant permission to use AI in an assessment; check the written tool policy.
- Review deadlines throughout the year. Watch both cohort programs and roles opened for immediate team needs.
- If you need income sooner, assess adjacent roles and contract terms against your needs and authorization. Accepting an interim role does not stop you from building toward a longer-term goal.
