An internship is one kind of evidence, not an entry requirement for every entry-level job. If you do not have one, your task is to show the same things an internship would have shown: that you can do the core work, finish what you start, work with other people and learn on the job. This guide is about finding that evidence in what you already did, presenting it honestly, and pointing it at employers whose postings ask for what you can show.

## What an internship would have proved

Before you look for substitutes, name what is missing. A hiring team reading an internship line usually learns four things:

- **You have done the work in a real setting**, with deadlines and someone else's standards.
- **You can work in a team**, take feedback and communicate progress.
- **Someone vouched for you**, through a manager, a supervisor or a return offer.
- **You know what the job involves**, so you are less likely to be surprised by it.

Each of these can be shown another way. None of them requires the word "intern".

## Evidence that can replace it

Go through each source below and write down what you actually did. Skip any that do not apply.

- **Projects.** A finished project in the target role's domain is the closest substitute for real work. [New grad projects that count](../new-grad-projects/) covers what helps and what does not; for software roles, an [open-source contribution](../open-source-contributions/) also shows you can work in someone else's codebase.
- **Research and lab work.** A research assistantship, a lab position or a thesis shows sustained work under supervision, method, and writing. A named supervisor can also be a reference.
- **Teaching and tutoring.** A TA, grader or tutor role shows you can explain material, handle many people's questions and meet grading deadlines.
- **Part-time and campus jobs.** Retail, food service, front-desk, warehouse and call-center work show reliability, customer contact and working under pressure. For sales, operations, healthcare and customer-facing roles these are often directly relevant.
- **Leadership and organizing.** Running a club, an event, a budget or a volunteer team shows planning and coordination. Be specific about your part.
- **Coursework with a real deliverable.** A capstone, a design project for an outside client, a case competition or a clinical placement counts when you can describe what you produced.

::: evidence official
If you need standard wording for what a part-time job involved, O*NET's occupation pages list typical tasks and transferable skills; the [retail salespersons entry](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/41-2031.00) lists tasks such as recommending merchandise based on customer needs. Use it for vocabulary, and only claim the tasks you actually did.
:::

## An evidence inventory

Fill in one row per item before you write or rewrite your résumé. The rows below are illustrations, not achievements to copy.

| What you did | What it shows | Who can confirm it | Roles it supports |
|---|---|---|---|
| Graded for an intro statistics course, two terms | Explaining methods, deadlines, accuracy | Course instructor | Data analyst, teaching, training |
| Shift lead at a campus café | Scheduling, handling complaints, cash handling | Store manager | Operations, retail management trainee, customer success |
| Capstone: inventory tool for a local nonprofit | Requirements gathering, delivering to a real user | Nonprofit contact, faculty advisor | Software, IT, business analyst |
| Undergraduate research in a soil lab | Sampling protocol, lab safety, data recording | Principal investigator | Lab technician, environmental, quality |

Then run each line through [the résumé evidence test](../resume-evidence-test/): can you prove it, explain it and reproduce it? Anything that fails gets rewritten or removed.

## How to present it

- **Use a plain "Experience" section.** Paid part-time work, research and TA positions are experience. Give each the title you held, the employer or department, and dates.
- **Lead with what transfers.** "Resolved customer billing disputes and logged them for the store manager" is more useful to an operations hiring team than "Cashier".
- **Put a "Projects" section where it helps.** For roles where projects are the main evidence, place it above part-time work. For customer-facing roles, the reverse may be stronger.
- **Do not relabel.** A class project is not a contract, and a club role is not employment. Describe it accurately; the substance carries the weight.

When an interviewer asks why you did not intern, give a short, true answer and move to the evidence: "I worked through school to pay tuition, so I don't have an internship. In that job I…" or "I spent my summers in a research lab instead. The work there was…". You do not owe more detail than that.

## Who to target

Some employers and postings fit candidates without internships better than others. Read the posting rather than assuming:

- **Postings that ask for 0 years or "0–2 years"**, and that list skills you can evidence. [Are you qualified?](../are-you-qualified/) separates firm from soft requirements.
- **Structured entry programs**: graduate schemes, [rotational and development programs](../rotational-and-development-programs/), and management trainee tracks are built to train people from the start. Check each program's eligibility and dates.
- **Employers in your region or sector** that are less visible on campus. [Build a target-company list](../build-a-target-company-list/) explains how to find and tier them.
- **Unlevelled or oddly titled roles** the board's strict rules may leave out. [Finding hidden new grad roles](../finding-hidden-new-grad-roles/) shows how to use EXPLORE to find them, and how to check what level they really are.

If a posting says it requires prior internship experience, take it at its word, or ask the recruiter whether other experience counts before you spend time on a tailored application.

## A one-week plan

1. Fill in the evidence inventory and run the evidence test on every row.
2. Rewrite your résumé with an "Experience" section and, if relevant, a "Projects" section.
3. Ask one supervisor, instructor or manager whether they would be a reference.
4. Draft your 20-second answer to "why no internship?" and say it out loud.
5. Build or refresh your target list, then check the board's [new this week](../../jobs/new-this-week/) page and apply to postings that match your evidence.
