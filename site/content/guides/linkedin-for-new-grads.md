LinkedIn is a profile and a search engine before it is a social network. Set it up as if a recruiter were going to read it for thirty seconds — because one will.

## Headline

The headline is the line that appears in search results and connection requests. Use it to say the role you want, not the one you had or the one you are studying.

- Good: `CS new grad · interested in backend & distributed systems · open to SWE roles`
- Less useful: `Student at the University of Somewhere · Aspiring Software Engineer · Open to opportunities`

Include a city or region if you will not relocate, plus a visa-status note if it is relevant — see [Visa sponsorship and citizenship flags](../visa-sponsorship-and-citizenship/) for language that is honest and recruiter-readable.

## About

Three short paragraphs. What you have done, what you want, and what you read or build outside of class. No buzzwords. No "passionate about" or "driven by". A recruiter scans for tools, project types, and industries; a hiring manager scans for clarity and honesty.

## Experience and projects

Label class and side projects accurately as projects, with dates and your contribution; do not present them as employment. Each gets a one-line description, a bullet list of what you did and what changed, and the stack you used. The format is the same as the résumé bullets in [Resume bullet points](../resume-bullet-points/).

## Open to Work

Choose Open to Work visibility deliberately. The public option adds a photo frame; recruiter-only visibility shares preferences with LinkedIn Recruiter users and does not add that public frame. LinkedIn says complete privacy is not guaranteed. Set accurate job titles, locations and availability. In India you can also specify notice period and expected annual salary. See [LinkedIn's current instructions](https://www.linkedin.com/help/linkedin/answer/a507508/let-recruiters-know-you-re-open-to-work).

## Discoverability

Recruiters search by skill, school, and previous employer. Add skills you would actually defend in an interview, not every keyword that applies to you. Endorse classmates; ask two classmates and one former manager to endorse you back.

## Connection requests

Send them to alumni, recruiters at target companies, and people who work on teams you would join. Always include a one-line note. A short relevant note can explain the connection; platform limits and recipient preferences vary.

You do not need to post, comment, or build a personal brand. A complete profile that recruiters can find is enough. Read [Messaging recruiters](../messaging-recruiters/) for what to write once a recruiter accepts.
