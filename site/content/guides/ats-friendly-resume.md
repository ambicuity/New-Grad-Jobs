Almost every job on this board is hosted on one of four applicant tracking systems: Greenhouse, Lever, Ashby and Workday. They parse your résumé into fields before a person sees it, so the goal is a document that parses cleanly and reads well, in that order.

## Formatting that parses

- One column. Multi-column layouts and text boxes are read in the wrong order or dropped.
- Standard section headings: Education, Experience, Projects, Skills. Parsers look for these words.
- Real text, not images. Logos, icons and scanned pages are invisible to the parser.
- A PDF exported from a text editor. A PDF made from a screenshot or a design tool is often an image.
- Dates in one format, with a month and a year, on every entry.
- No tables for layout. Simple bullet points survive; nested tables do not.

## Content that matches

Recruiters and screening tools both look for the posting's own words. Mirror the exact technologies, methods and role names the posting uses where they are true of you. If the posting says "data pipelines" and your project built one, say "data pipeline", not "ETL system".

Lead each bullet with what you did and what it produced, and quantify only what you can defend in an interview. A number you cannot explain is worse than no number.

## Length and order

New grad résumés are one page. Put Education first while you are still a student or within a year of graduating, then Experience, then Projects. If your projects are stronger than your internships, swap them.

## Before you submit

- Open the PDF in a plain viewer and select all: does the text select in reading order?
- Paste the résumé into a text file: is anything missing or scrambled?
- Fill the application form fields yourself rather than trusting the autofill; parsers are good but not perfect, and the form is what the recruiter sees.

## One résumé per role family, not per job

Keep a base version and a tailored variant per family of roles (software, data, hardware, business). Tailoring the summary and the top few bullets is enough; rewriting the whole document for every application costs time that is better spent applying earlier.
