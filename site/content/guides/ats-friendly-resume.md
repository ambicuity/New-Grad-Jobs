Employers use applicant tracking systems to store applications and may parse résumés into searchable fields. Parsing, screening rules and human review are different steps. A parsing error does not automatically mean rejection: [Greenhouse documents that the résumé remains attached](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse). Aim for clear text and readable formatting, then check the application fields.

## Formatting that parses

- One column. Multi-column layouts and text boxes can cause reading-order or extraction problems.
- Standard section headings: Education, Experience, Projects, Skills. Parsers look for these words.
- Use real text for essential information. Image-only résumés may not parse, and icons should not replace contact details or headings.
- Use the file type the application requests, usually a text-based PDF or DOCX. Check that the text can be selected; the tool used to create the file does not by itself determine whether it is readable.
- Dates in one format, with a month and a year, on every entry.
- No tables for layout. Simple bullet points reduce extraction problems; complex tables can scramble reading order.

## Content that matches

Recruiters and screening tools both look for the posting's own words. Mirror the exact technologies, methods and role names the posting uses where they are true of you. If the posting says "data pipelines" and your project built one, say "data pipeline", not "ETL system".

Lead each bullet with what you did and what it produced, and quantify only what you can defend in an interview. A number you cannot explain is worse than no number.

## Length and order

One page is a useful default for a new grad résumé; follow field-specific conventions and the application instructions. Put Education first while you are still a student or within a year of graduating, then Experience, then Projects. If your projects are stronger than your internships, swap them.

## Before you submit

- Open the PDF in a plain viewer and select all: does the text select in reading order?
- Paste the résumé into a text file: is anything missing or scrambled?
- Fill the application form fields yourself rather than trusting the autofill; parsers are good but not perfect, and recruiters may review both the fields and the attached document.

## One résumé per role family, not per job

Keep a base version and a tailored variant per family of roles (software, data, hardware, business). Tailoring the summary and the top few bullets is enough; rewriting the whole document for every application costs time that is better spent applying earlier.
