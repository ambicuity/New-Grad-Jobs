Use feedback and your own observations to identify preparation gaps. Rejection alone rarely reveals the cause, and headcount, eligibility, or another candidate's fit may affect the outcome.

## Map where you are losing

If you have done three or more onsites and gotten no offer, write down for each one which round ended it. Possible areas to investigate:

- Always the recruiter screen → review your role explanation, eligibility, and logistics.
- Always the technical round → check the relevant technical skills, accuracy, and communication against any feedback.
- Always the behavioral or values round → review whether your examples answered the question and showed your own contribution.
- Different rounds each time → avoid inferring one cause; compare concrete observations and employer feedback.

Each pattern has a different fix.

## Always the recruiter screen

The recruiter screen is fifteen minutes of motivation, logistics, and a two-minute summary of who you are. Three common misses:

- **The summary is too vague.** "I'm a CS student who likes to code" is not a summary. Two minutes of the project you are most proud of, what you want next, and why this role. Rehearse it; see [Behavioral interviews](../behavioral-interviews/).
- **You are not aligned with the role.** If the recruiter asks "why this role?" and you say "I just want any new-grad role", the answer ends the screen. Read the team's page; mention one specific thing.
- **Logistics block you.** Salary, location, start date. The recruiter is checking whether they can hire you. Decide these before the call.

## Always the technical round

If you reach the technical round and fail it twice, the problem is rarely "you cannot code." Investigate areas such as:

- **You go silent.** Interviewers score on what you say while you think. See [How to handle an interview question you don't know](../how-to-handle-an-interview-question-you-dont-know/) and [How to prepare for an interview in 7 days](../how-to-prepare-for-an-interview-in-7-days/).
- **You solve a different problem.** The interviewer often steers you; if you do not adjust, you miss the round.
- **You do not test your code.** Two off-by-one errors that a thirty-second trace would catch lose the round. Always trace one or two examples before you say you are done.

Practice is the fix. Time-box yourself on a LeetCode problem for thirty-five minutes, narrate out loud, and trace one example. See [LeetCode roadmap for new grads](../leetcode-roadmap-for-new-grads/).

## Always the behavioral or values round

One possible gap: stories omit context, actions, or results. A two-minute answer that hits STAR and ends with a reflection is harder than it sounds; see [Behavioral interviews](../behavioral-interviews/) for the structure and the reflection step most candidates skip.

Another possible gap is rehearsing wording so closely that you do not answer the question asked. Give a specific, honest example, including an unsuccessful result when relevant.

## Different rounds each time

This is the hardest pattern. You are clearing individual bars but not aggregating to a hire. Two things to try:

- **Ask the recruiter.** After an onsite, ask "what feedback can you share?" Some will; some will not. Any feedback is data.
- **Pick a smaller role to convert.** If you cannot convert at the largest companies on your list, convert at a smaller one. Read [Startup vs enterprise](../startup-vs-enterprise/) for the trade-off, and [How to evaluate an offer](../evaluating-an-offer/) for what to look for.

## When it is the loop as a whole

"We went with another candidate" does not identify a cause. If your own notes show a recurring communication gap, test it with a mock. Ask a friend to do a full mock loop with you, then sit with you for the feedback. Possible practice targets include: pace (too fast or too slow), confidence (you apologise more than you explain), or specificity (your answers are general when they should be about you).

If the data says you have done five loops with no offers, change something material. Five outcomes are a small sample. Change preparation when you have concrete evidence of a gap; do not treat the count as proof of a personal failing.

## After each interview

Use a per-interview postmortem to capture what happened and what you would do differently. See [How to learn from every interview](../interview-postmortem/) for a copyable template. Do not try to fix everything at once; pick one to three improvements and change one cycle before the next attempt.
