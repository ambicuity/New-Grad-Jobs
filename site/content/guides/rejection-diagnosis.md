A rejection email that says "we decided not to move forward" is honest but useless. Students regularly walk away thinking "I failed because of the ATS", "I failed because I'm international", or "I failed because I didn't do enough LeetCode" — and each of those is a guess. This guide is about replacing the guess with what you actually know, and being honest about the difference.

::: evidence primary
NGJ applies the [configured inclusion rules](https://github.com/ambicuity/New-Grad-Jobs/blob/main/config.yml) to postings. Sponsorship and citizenship phrases are flags, not automatic exclusions from the curated set. A board classification cannot establish why an employer rejected your application.
:::

## Observation is not explanation

You applied. They rejected you. The gap between those two facts is where the guesswork happens. Be honest about what you observed and what you inferred.

- You **observed**: they sent a rejection email.
- You **inferred**: the reason.

The inference is the part to question. A common cognitive shortcut is to assume the most painful explanation is true — that the rejection was personal, structural, or caused by some trait of yours. A generic rejection cannot establish or rule out any of those explanations.

## Evidence you have

This is the list of things you can point to with confidence. Anything not on this list is inference.

- The rejection email itself, verbatim. (Most say "we decided not to move forward" or "we will not be moving forward with your application".)
- Recruiter feedback, if they offered it.
- Interview feedback, if the recruiter shared it after asking.
- Your assessment score, if the platform showed one.
- Your résumé as submitted.
- The job description as posted.
- Your interview notes, written while the round was still fresh.

If you have none of those, you have very little. That's not failure; that's a sample size of one.

## Evidence you don't have

This is the list of reasons the rejection might have been caused by something you cannot see from the outside. Naming these out loud makes the guess go away.

- An internal candidate was already selected.
- The recruiter's pipeline was full and your application never reached a human.
- A hiring freeze was announced after your interview but before your offer.
- A competing candidate had a closer match to the role.
- Headcount changed.
- An internal referral arrived after your application did.
- The hiring manager's preference shifted.
- The requisition was paused or cancelled.

These are possible explanations, not findings about your application or a ranking by frequency. Some concern employer decisions; others concern relative fit.

## What "the ATS rejected me" usually means

The most common self-blame story after a rejection is "the ATS rejected me". The rejection email alone does not establish that. There are two distinct mechanisms and they are often confused.

- **Auto-reject from application answers.** Some employers configure automatic rejection from specific application answers (eligibility, graduation date, years of experience). [Greenhouse documents this mechanism](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/360000653472-Auto-reject). It is _not_ résumé parsing.
- **Résumé parsing.** The résumé is parsed into structured fields and stored. [Greenhouse notes that an unsuccessful parse does not remove the résumé](https://support.greenhouse.io/hc/en-us/articles/200989175-Unsuccessful-resume-parse); it remains attached.

A missing exact title from a parsed résumé is not evidence that an algorithm rejected you. See [An ATS-friendly résumé](../ats-friendly-resume/) and [Why applications get rejected](../why-applications-get-rejected/) for the actual mechanisms.

## A diagnostic protocol

When you receive a rejection, run this protocol before changing anything.

1. **Write down what you actually know.** The rejection email, verbatim. Any feedback. Your notes from the loop. The date you applied. The date you heard back.
2. **List the explanations that fit the evidence.** Use the "evidence you have" list. If you cannot name a reason from that list, you do not have one.
3. **List the explanations that are also consistent with the evidence.** Use the "evidence you don't have" list. Internal candidate, hiring freeze, role pause. Any of these fits "they said no" without any evidence about you.
4. **For each candidate explanation, ask: what action would I take if this were true?** If the action is the same — improve one thing and re-apply — then the diagnosis does not change your plan and is not worth more time.
5. **Choose one change that survives the test above.** Pick the smallest, most controllable change. Do not change everything at once.

## What you can do about the parts you do control

The protocol above separates what you observed from what you inferred. The action is on the inferred parts you can influence.

- **The résumé did not match the role.** Tailor the next application. See [Tailoring your résumé](../tailoring-your-resume/).
- **You underperformed in a specific interview section.** Drill that section. See [How to learn from every interview](../interview-postmortem/) and the relevant preparation guide ([Coding interviews](../coding-interviews/), [Behavioral interviews](../behavioral-interviews/), [System design](../system-design/)).
- **You had no referrals.** Build a referral habit. See [How to ask for a referral](../how-to-ask-for-a-referral/).
- **Your application count was high and your interview count was low.** Diagnose the funnel. See [Diagnose your job-search funnel](../diagnose-your-job-search-funnel/) and [No interviews after 100 applications](../no-interviews-after-100-applications/).

## What you cannot do about the rest

You cannot control whether the employer prefers an internal candidate. You cannot out-apply a hiring freeze. You cannot out-LeetCode a requisition cancellation. Recognising that is not defeat; it is the difference between a controllable improvement and a self-blame spiral.

::: evidence practical
If you cannot name an actionable change that distinguishes this rejection from other applications, the rejection did not give you new information. Move on without a diagnosis.
:::
