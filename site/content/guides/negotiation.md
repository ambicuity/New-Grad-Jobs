New grad offers are more negotiable than most graduates assume and less negotiable than the internet suggests. The bands are narrow, the levers are few, and the way you ask matters more than how much. This guide is about asking well.

## What is usually negotiable

- **Signing bonus.** The easiest lever; it does not change the band and it does not recur.
- **Base, within the band.** Large employers have a range per level; you can ask to move within it, usually with a competing offer or a location argument.
- **Equity**, sometimes, at companies that grant it.
- **Start date and relocation.** Often flexible and worth asking about.

## What is usually not

- **Level.** New grad is a level; you will not be hired above it without experience.
- **Base above the band.** Nobody at a large employer can do this for a new grad.
- **Cohort program terms.** Structured programs pay the class the same; asking is fine, expect a no.

## What you need first

A written offer, a real reason, and a number. Reasons that work: a competing offer (the strongest), a posted range or level band you are below, cost of living at the location, or a specific skill the posting valued that you bring. "I would like more" is not a reason; "another offer is at X and I would prefer to join you" is.

## The script

Send it by email so there is a record and no one is put on the spot, then follow up by phone if they prefer.

> Thank you for the offer. I am excited about the team and the work, and I want to make this work. I have a competing offer with a base of X and a signing bonus of Y. If you can bring the base to X, I will accept today.

Three parts: enthusiasm, a specific ask with a reason, and a commitment. The commitment is what makes recruiters go and get the number; a request with no commitment is a shopping expedition, and they treat it as one. Only say "I will accept" if you will.

Ask once, for one thing or one package. A second round is possible if the first was declined and you have something new to add, but three rounds signals that you will not accept anything.

## If they say no

Ask whether anything else is possible: signing bonus, start date, an earlier review. Then decide on the offer as it stands with [Evaluating an offer](../evaluating-an-offer/). A polite no is not a reason to walk away from a good job, and a good employer will not withdraw an offer because you asked politely. If one does, you have learned something.

## International candidates

Negotiation does not affect sponsorship policy, and it does not help to try to trade salary for a visa petition. Settle the immigration questions first; see [OPT and STEM OPT](../opt-and-stem-opt/).

## Things not to do

- Invent or inflate a competing offer
- Negotiate before the written offer
- Negotiate over the phone without notes
- Accept, then reopen
- Apologize for asking. It is expected.
