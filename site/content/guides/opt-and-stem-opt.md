This is orientation, not legal advice. Immigration rules change and your situation is specific. The information chain for immigration questions should be:

1. Read this orientation to identify questions.
2. Check the official DHS / USCIS sources.
3. Discuss your program and deadlines with your university Designated School Official (DSO).
4. Consult a qualified immigration attorney when necessary.

NGJ's role is to help you know what to ask and where to look. Your DSO is the right next step for questions about your program, your I-20, and the timing of your application. A qualified immigration attorney is the right next step for legal questions about your specific case.

## What OPT is

Optional Practical Training lets an F-1 student work in the United States in a job related to their field of study. Post-completion OPT is the one new graduates use: a period of work authorization, typically twelve months, that begins after the program ends. You apply through the DSO, who recommends it on your I-20, and then to USCIS for the employment authorization document (EAD). Timing matters: there is a window before and after program completion in which the application must be filed, and unemployment limits apply: generally 90 days during initial post-completion OPT, or 150 days in total including a STEM extension. Confirm both with your DSO early, because the application takes time to process and you cannot begin initial OPT employment before USCIS approves your authorization and the authorized start date arrives. A pending application alone is not permission to work. See [8 CFR 214.2(f)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/subpart-A/section-214.2).

## The STEM extension

Graduates of degrees on the government's STEM designated degree list can apply for an extension of OPT, typically twenty-four months, giving more time to work and more chances at a longer-term visa. The extension has conditions that plain OPT does not: the employer must be enrolled in E-Verify, there is a formal training plan (Form I-983) that the employer signs, and there are reporting obligations during the extension. Confirm E-Verify enrollment and willingness to complete the training plan before accepting. A timely, properly filed STEM extension can permit continued employment for up to 180 days while it is pending; have your DSO and employer confirm your eligibility and documents. See [8 CFR 214.2(f)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/subpart-A/section-214.2).

## What "sponsorship" means in this context

Initial OPT generally does not require an employer visa petition, but STEM OPT requires employer participation in E-Verify and Form I-983. Longer-term employment may require a different immigration route, such as an employer H-1B petition. Cap-subject H-1B cases have registration and selection requirements; some qualifying employers or cases are cap-exempt. Check the current [USCIS H-1B guidance](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations) with the employer’s immigration team. A "no sponsorship now or in the future" policy may also exclude candidates working temporarily on OPT. Ask whether the employer accepts your current authorization and future needs before applying. The board flags stated restrictions; [Sponsorship language](../sponsorship-language/) explains the phrases.

## Questions to ask an employer

1. Do you hire candidates on OPT?
2. Are you enrolled in E-Verify? (Needed for the STEM extension.)
3. Have you sponsored H-1B petitions for people in this role before, and what is your policy?
4. If a later immigration application or selection is unsuccessful, what options, if any, would the employer support? A transfer abroad requires its own role, authorization and approval; it is not guaranteed.

Ask the recruiter at the first screen. It is a normal question and the answer saves everyone time.

## Using the board

Filter with "no restriction stated" to drop postings that state they will not sponsor or require citizenship. The remaining postings have not said either thing, which is not a promise, so still ask the questions above. U.S. personnel security clearances require U.S. citizenship, while contractor and export-control eligibility depends on the role; see [Citizenship and clearance](../citizenship-and-clearance/).

A "no restriction stated" chip is not a positive signal of sponsorship; it is the absence of a stated restriction. It does not change your eligibility or the employer's policy. Treat it as a filter, not an answer.

## Keep your own calendar

Program end date, OPT application window, employment authorization start, the unemployment-day count, the STEM extension deadline, and the H-1B registration period each year. Write them down at the start of the search. Confirm the dates with your DSO and keep evidence of your employment and reporting.

## Official DHS and USCIS sources

For authoritative, current information, use the official pages directly. The links below are the starting points; the pages link out to forms, processing times, and policy guidance.

- **[USCIS Optional Practical Training](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/optional-practical-training-opt-for-f-1-students).** The USCIS page on F-1 OPT, including eligibility, application steps, and the EAD.
- **[USCIS STEM OPT](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/optional-practical-training-extension-for-stem-students-stem-opt).** The USCIS page on the 24-month STEM extension, including Form I-983 and reporting requirements.
- **[USCIS Policy Manual](https://www.uscis.gov/policy-manual).** The authoritative guidance USCIS officers follow; useful when a page summary is not enough.
- **[DHS Study in the States](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/).** The DHS resource for F-1 students, including OPT, STEM OPT, and Form I-983.
- **[DHS Form I-983 information](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/form-i-983-overview).** The training plan used by STEM OPT students and their employers.
- **[E-Verify](https://www.e-verify.gov/).** Information on the employer-side E-Verify program; employees can use myE-Verify to check their own records.
- **[USCIS I-9 Central](https://www.uscis.gov/i-9-central).** The employment eligibility verification form every U.S. employer uses.
- **[myE-Verify](https://www.e-verify.gov/employees/mye-verify).** The employee-facing side of E-Verify for self-checks.
- **[USCIS Processing Times](https://egov.uscis.gov/processing-times/).** Current processing times for OPT, STEM OPT, and related applications.

Bookmark these pages and re-read them when something changes in your situation; the rules update more often than you would expect.

## What this guide is not

NGJ is not a substitute for the official sources above, your DSO, or an attorney. Specific questions — which STEM program codes apply to your degree, whether a particular job counts as related to your field, how a gap in employment affects your unemployment-day count, what to do if your OPT is not approved by your start date — should go to your DSO and, where appropriate, an attorney. This guide's role is to help you know what to ask and where to look next.
