This is orientation, not legal advice. Immigration rules change and your situation is specific. Your university's international student office (the DSO) and the official USCIS and SEVP pages are the sources of truth; use this page to know what to ask them.

## What OPT is

Optional Practical Training lets an F-1 student work in the United States in a job related to their field of study. Post-completion OPT is the one new graduates use: a period of work authorization, typically twelve months, that begins after the program ends. You apply through the DSO, who recommends it on your I-20, and then to USCIS for the employment authorization document. Timing matters: there is a window before and after program completion in which the application must be filed, and there is a limit on days spent unemployed while on OPT. Confirm both with your DSO early, because the application takes time to process and you cannot start work until the document is approved.

## The STEM extension

Graduates of degrees on the government's STEM designated degree list can apply for an extension of OPT, typically twenty-four months, giving more time to work and more chances at a longer-term visa. The extension has conditions the plain OPT does not: the employer must be enrolled in the E-Verify program, there is a formal training plan (Form I-983) that the employer signs, and there are reporting obligations during the extension. Not every employer is enrolled in E-Verify, and small companies often are not, so this is a question to ask before accepting an offer.

## What "sponsorship" means in this context

Working on OPT does not require the employer to sponsor anything; the authorization is yours. Sponsorship becomes relevant when OPT runs out and the employer would need to petition for a work visa such as the H-1B, which is subject to an annual lottery and a filing cost. When a posting says "will not sponsor now or in the future", it usually means the employer will hire on OPT but will not file that later petition. Some employers will not even do that. The board flags stated restrictions; [Sponsorship language](../sponsorship-language/) explains the phrases.

## Questions to ask an employer

1. Do you hire candidates on OPT?
2. Are you enrolled in E-Verify? (Needed for the STEM extension.)
3. Have you sponsored H-1B petitions for people in this role before, and what is your policy?
4. If the H-1B lottery does not select me, what happens? Some large employers have options such as transferring to an office abroad; most small ones do not.

Ask the recruiter at the first screen. It is a normal question and the answer saves everyone time.

## Using the board

Filter with "no restriction stated" to drop postings that state they will not sponsor or require citizenship. The remaining postings have not said either thing, which is not a promise, so still ask the questions above. Government contractors and roles requiring a security clearance almost always require citizenship; see [Citizenship and clearance](../citizenship-and-clearance/).

## Keep your own calendar

Program end date, OPT application window, employment authorization start, the unemployment-day count, the STEM extension deadline, and the H-1B registration period each year. Write them down at the start of the search. Missing one is the most common and most avoidable problem international graduates face.
