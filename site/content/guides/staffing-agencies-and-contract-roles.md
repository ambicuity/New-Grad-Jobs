Staffing agencies, recruiters and contract work are legal and common. Some graduates choose them on purpose. NGJ aims to list permanent roles posted by the real employer, so it leaves most of that work out. This guide explains what the board does, what it cannot catch, and how to read the arrangement behind a posting before you agree to anything.

## What the board does, and what it cannot do

NGJ's behaviour comes from its published [configuration](https://github.com/ambicuity/New-Grad-Jobs/blob/main/config.yml):

1. **Direct-employer sources.** The board reads career sites chosen from direct employers. Staffing agencies and recruiters are not added as sources.
2. **Title rules.** A posting is dropped when its title says temporary, temp, fixed-term, contract-to-hire or "N-month contract", has a standalone contract tag such as "(Contract)", or says part-time, seasonal or a fractional FTE such as ".3FTE". A title that is about contracts as a subject, such as "Contracts Administrator", stays.
3. **Indeed results are not filtered by employer type.** The board also collects Indeed results through JobSpy. An agency posting, or a contract role described only in the body, can still appear.

So check who the employer is and what the employment terms are for every posting, including on this board. [What counts as a new grad job?](../what-counts-as-a-new-grad-job/) describes the rest of the inclusion rules.

## How to recognise the arrangement behind a posting

Each cue below is a reason to ask a question. None of them proves anything on its own.

### Agency or recruiter postings

- The company is unnamed: "our client", "a leading bank", "a Fortune 500 company".
- The poster's name differs from the business you would work in, and the posting says you will be placed or deployed there.
- The application goes to a recruiter's site rather than the employer's own careers page.

An agency may be your legal employer, with pay and benefits coming from the agency, while the work is done for its client.

### Contract, temporary and contract-to-hire

- The body gives an end date, a duration ("12 months"), or an hourly rate with "W-2", "1099" or "corp-to-corp" language.
- "Contract-to-hire" or "temp-to-perm" means a conversion is possible, not promised. Ask what decides it and when.
- "Contractor" can mean self-employment, which is a different legal relationship from being an employee. Ask how you will be classified and paid.

### Train-and-deploy and training bonds

Some firms recruit graduates, train them, and then place them with clients. Some of these arrangements, and some ordinary employers, ask you to repay training costs if you leave before a set date.

::: evidence official
The US Consumer Financial Protection Bureau reported in 2023 that [some companies require workers to agree to pay back training costs if they leave](https://www.consumerfinance.gov/data-research/research-reports/issue-spotlight-consumer-risks-posed-by-employer-driven-debt/full-report/), calling these training repayment agreement provisions (TRAPs). The report names nursing, including new-graduate programs, along with trucking, retail, aviation and financial services among the fields where they appear.
:::

A repayment clause is not automatically unlawful, and the rules on it differ by state, province and country, and change. Read the wording, and if it is unclear or the amount is large, ask an employment lawyer or your local labour authority before you sign.

## Questions to ask before you apply or sign

1. Who will be my legal employer, who pays me, and who provides benefits?
2. Where will I work, and who assigns that? Can it change?
3. Is the role permanent, or does it end on a set date? If it is contract-to-hire, what decides conversion, and when?
4. Will I be an employee or an independent contractor?
5. Is there a training period? Is it paid? Does it carry a repayment obligation, a service bond or a penalty for leaving?
6. If there is a repayment clause, what is the exact amount, how does it reduce over time, and does it apply if I am let go?
7. For international candidates: who files any work-authorization paperwork? See [Visa sponsorship and citizenship flags](../visa-sponsorship-and-citizenship/).

Get the answers in writing. [Reading your employment agreement](../reading-your-employment-agreement/) covers the rest of the document, and [Questions to ask before accepting](../questions-before-accepting/) covers the wider offer.

## Fees and red flags

You should not pay to get a job.

::: evidence official
The FTC says [honest employers will never ask you to pay to get a job](https://consumer.ftc.gov/articles/job-scams) and that honest placement firms do not typically charge job candidates a fee. If a placement firm asks for a fee, especially in advance, the FTC says to walk away.
:::

Rules on agency fees vary by jurisdiction. Ontario, for example, says a temporary help agency [cannot charge an assignment employee a fee](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/temporary-help-agencies) for becoming its employee, for being assigned work, or for help with résumés or interview preparation. It also cannot stop a client from hiring the employee directly. In India, the government's [National Career Service](https://www.ncs.gov.in/) says its services are free and that jobseekers should not pay fees to employers.

Stop and verify if you see any of these:

- a fee to register, to be assigned, or to start training
- a security deposit, or original certificates held by the employer
- a repayment amount you are not shown until after you accept
- pressure to sign the same day
- an offer with no named employer, or one you cannot verify independently

[How to detect a fake job or recruiter](../how-to-detect-a-fake-job-or-recruiter/) gives the verification steps and where to report a scam in the US, Canada and India.

## If you choose agency or contract work

Contract and agency roles can be a reasonable bridge, and this guide does not argue against them. If you take one, keep the written terms, track the end date, and keep applying for permanent roles if that is your goal. Mark the role on your own list and check back on the board's [new this week](../../jobs/new-this-week/) page for permanent openings.
