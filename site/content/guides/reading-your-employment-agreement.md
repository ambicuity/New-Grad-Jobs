This guide is for anyone about to sign an offer letter, employment agreement, or the confidentiality and invention agreement that often comes with it. Read the documents in full before you sign; the offer call and the documents can say different things. Laws on these clauses differ by state, province and country, and the wording of your document matters. For anything you are unsure about, ask HR to explain it in writing, and consider asking an employment attorney. This is educational information, not legal advice.

Deciding whether the offer is right for you is covered in [Evaluating an offer](../evaluating-an-offer/) and [Questions to ask before accepting](../questions-before-accepting/). This guide is about the text.

## Employment terms and start date

- **At-will or term language.** Many US offer letters say either side can end employment at any time, with or without cause or notice. Some agreements set a notice period instead. Note which you have and whether anything in the document limits it.
- **Start date and contingencies.** List every condition: background check, references, degree completion, work authorization, a signed confidentiality agreement. Ask what happens if a condition is late rather than failed. [Background checks and pre-employment verification](../background-checks-and-pre-employment-verification/) covers the screening and your consent to it; read the consent form as carefully as the letter.
- **Title, location and schedule.** Check the job title, work location, remote or hybrid terms, and whether the employer can change them.
- **Probation.** Some agreements set a probation period with different notice or benefits. Note its length and what changes after it.

## Pay, bonuses and repayment

- **Base pay.** Check the amount, the pay period, and whether the role is described as exempt or non-exempt from overtime in the US.
- **Bonus terms.** Is an annual bonus guaranteed, a target, or fully discretionary? Do you have to be employed on the payment date to receive it? Is a first-year bonus prorated?
- **Signing bonus and relocation repayment.** Some agreements require you to repay some or all of a signing bonus or relocation payment if you leave within a set period. Find the period, whether repayment is full or prorated, whether it applies if the employer ends your employment, and whether the employer can deduct it from your final pay. Whether a deduction like that is allowed depends on where you work.
- **Severance.** The US Department of Labor says "there is no requirement in the Fair Labor Standards Act (FLSA) for severance pay"; it is "a matter of agreement" ([DOL](https://www.dol.gov/general/topic/wages/severancepay)). If the letter says nothing, assume nothing.

[Understanding compensation](../understanding-compensation/) explains how each pay component works.

## Equity

If the offer includes stock options, restricted stock units or other equity, the offer letter is usually a summary. The binding terms are in the equity plan and the grant agreement, which may arrive after you start. Ask for both, and read for:

- **Grant size and type**, and whether the number is shares or a dollar value converted at a future price.
- **Vesting schedule.** Equity usually vests on a schedule set in the grant agreement; check the total period, any cliff before the first portion vests, and how often it vests after that.
- **What happens if you leave**, including how long you have to exercise options and whether unvested equity is forfeited.
- **Board approval.** Grants at some companies must be approved after you start; the letter may say "subject to approval."

## Restrictive covenants and confidentiality

- **Non-compete.** A clause limiting where you can work after you leave.

::: evidence official
The FTC's [Noncompete Rule page](https://www.ftc.gov/legal-library/browse/rules/noncompete-rule) states: "The Noncompete Rule is not in effect and it is not enforceable." A district court order of August 20, 2024 stopped the FTC from enforcing it, and in [September 2025](https://www.ftc.gov/news-events/news/press-releases/2025/09/federal-trade-commission-files-accede-vacatur-non-compete-clause-rule) the FTC took steps to dismiss its appeals and accede to the rule's vacatur. The page also lists a February 12, 2026 Federal Register notice titled "Removal of the Non-Compete Rule To Conform These Rules to Federal Court Decisions."
:::

With no federal rule in force, whether a non-compete in your agreement is enforceable depends on state, provincial or national law and on its wording. The FTC can still act against specific non-compete practices case by case.

State and provincial law varies widely. For example, California's [Business and Professions Code section 16600](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=BPC&sectionNum=16600) says every contract restraining anyone "from engaging in a lawful profession, trade, or business of any kind is to that extent void," subject to exceptions in that chapter. Ontario's [Employment Standards Act guide](https://www.ontario.ca/document/your-guide-employment-standards-act-0/non-compete-agreements) says employers have been prohibited from entering into non-compete agreements since October 25, 2021, with exceptions for executives and the sale of a business. Elsewhere, ask a local attorney.

- **Non-solicit.** Limits on recruiting colleagues or approaching clients after you leave. Ontario's guide notes its ESA does not prohibit non-solicit or non-disclosure agreements.
- **Confidentiality.** What counts as confidential, how long the duty lasts, and what you must return when you leave.
- **Invention assignment.** These clauses assign to the employer rights in what you create. Check whether they reach work done on your own time, and whether there is a schedule where you list prior inventions or projects you already own. Some states limit these clauses.

::: evidence official
[California Labor Code section 2870](https://leginfo.legislature.ca.gov/faces/codes_displaySection.xhtml?lawCode=LAB&sectionNum=2870) says an assignment provision does not apply to an invention "developed entirely on his or her own time without using the employer's equipment, supplies, facilities, or trade secret information," except inventions that relate to the employer's business or actual or demonstrably anticipated research, or that result from work done for the employer.
:::

If you have side projects, open-source work or a startup idea, list them on the prior-inventions schedule and ask HR how the clause applies. [Open source contributions](../open-source-contributions/) is relevant if you contribute publicly.

## Disputes and arbitration

Some agreements require disputes to go to private arbitration instead of court, and some waive class or collective actions. Check what disputes are covered, who pays the arbitrator, where it happens, and whether you can opt out within a set time.

::: evidence official
The [Ending Forced Arbitration of Sexual Assault and Sexual Harassment Act of 2021](https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/PLAW-117publ90/html/PLAW-117publ90.htm) provides that, at the election of the person alleging the conduct, no predispute arbitration agreement or joint-action waiver is valid or enforceable for a case filed under federal, tribal or state law that relates to a sexual assault or sexual harassment dispute.
:::

Other limits on arbitration depend on the jurisdiction and the claim.

## Questions to ask before you sign

| Clause | Question to ask HR |
| --- | --- |
| Contingencies | Which conditions are still open, and what happens if one is delayed rather than failed? |
| Start date | Is the date fixed, and how would a change be confirmed? |
| Bonus | Is it guaranteed, a target or discretionary? Must I be employed on the payment date? |
| Signing bonus and relocation | How long is the repayment period? Is it prorated? Does it apply if I am laid off? |
| Equity | Can I see the plan and grant agreement? When is the grant approved? What is the vesting schedule? |
| Non-compete and non-solicit | Which state's or province's law governs this agreement, and how does the company apply this clause? |
| Invention assignment | Does this cover work on my own time and equipment? How do I list prior projects? |
| Arbitration | Is it mandatory? Is there an opt-out window? |
| Changes | If HR told me something on the phone that is not in the letter, can it be added in writing? |

Ask by email so the answers are written down. If an answer worries you, an employment attorney can review the documents before you sign. Keep signed copies of everything; [First 90 days](../first-90-days/) covers what to do with them once you start.
