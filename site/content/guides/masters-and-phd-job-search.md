A master's or PhD can open postings that ask for one, and it can change how an employer levels you. It does not automatically make you mid-level, and it does not rule you out of entry-level roles. This guide covers which postings fit an advanced-degree graduate, how titles and levels can differ, how to translate research into terms a hiring team reads, and the timing questions that matter most around a defense or a degree conferral date.

## Which postings fit

Read the education line in each posting, and sort what you find into three groups:

- **Postings that require an advanced degree.** Some roles are built for it: research scientist, applied scientist, some data science, statistics, economics and policy research roles, and licensed fields where a graduate degree is the route to practice. These postings typically state it directly, for example "MS or PhD required" or "PhD in a relevant field".
- **Postings that prefer one.** "Bachelor's required, master's preferred" means your degree is a plus, not a separate track.
- **Postings that do not mention one.** Some entry-level postings fall here. A master's graduate can apply on the same terms as anyone else, and the extra coursework or thesis is evidence like any other.

::: evidence official
Occupations differ in how much education new hires are expected to have. O*NET's [statisticians summary](https://www.onetonline.org/link/summary/15-2041.00) reports that the most common response for required education is a master's degree, and O*NET's [Job Zones](https://www.onetonline.org/help/online/zones) describe the highest zone as occupations where most require graduate school.
:::

Check the occupation you are targeting rather than assuming your degree is required, preferred or irrelevant. The posting decides for that role.

## Titles and levels

Employers handle advanced degrees in different ways, and a posting rarely says which:

- **The same entry title for everyone**, with the degree shown in the interview and possibly reflected in pay within the band.
- **A separate posting for advanced-degree graduates**, often with "PhD" or "MS" in the title, a research focus, or a distinct university-recruiting page.
- **A higher starting level** for some PhD hires, especially in research roles. Whether that applies is the employer's decision; ask the recruiter which level the role is and whether your degree affects it.

The board's curated rules read titles. A title such as "Research Scientist, PhD" with no configured entry-level signal is left off the main list and out of WIDEN SCOPE, and a title at level III or higher is moved behind WIDEN SCOPE. To look past those rules, open the EXPLORE tab and add your own INCLUDE word, such as "PhD" or "Research Scientist". [Finding hidden new grad roles](../finding-hidden-new-grad-roles/) explains how, and [How NGJ classifies jobs](../how-ngj-classifies-jobs/) lists the rules.

## Translate research into industry terms

A hiring team outside academia reads your thesis for what it shows about how you work. Rewrite each research item in terms of the problem, your method, the result and who used it.

| Academic phrasing | Industry phrasing |
|---|---|
| Dissertation on X | Ran a multi-year project on X: defined the question, designed the method, delivered results |
| Published in a peer-reviewed venue | Wrote up results that passed external expert review |
| Maintained the lab's data pipeline | Built and maintained the data pipeline a team of researchers relied on |
| Mentored undergraduate researchers | Supervised and trained junior team members |
| Presented at a conference | Presented technical work to a specialist audience |

Keep the publication list, but move it below a short summary of skills and outcomes for non-research roles. For research roles, the publication list may belong near the top. Every translated line still has to pass [the résumé evidence test](../resume-evidence-test/): do not turn "contributed to" into "led". If your research cannot be shared, describe the method and skills without confidential detail.

In interviews, expect to explain your research to someone outside your subfield in a few minutes, and to say what you would do differently. Prepare a short and a long version. [Behavioral interviews](../behavioral-interviews/) helps you turn research episodes into stories.

## Timing around a defense or conferral date

Advanced-degree timelines are less fixed than undergraduate ones, so build your own calendar:

- **Your realistic finish date.** Employers will ask when you can start. Give a date you can meet, and tell them if a defense date moves.
- **Degree conditions in the offer.** Some offers depend on completing the degree or verifying it. Read the offer and ask what proof is needed and when; see [Background checks and pre-employment verification](../background-checks-and-pre-employment-verification/).
- **Recruiting seasons.** Some employers hire advanced-degree candidates through campus cycles, others year-round. [The new-grad hiring timeline](../new-grad-hiring-timeline/) covers the seasons; check each employer's university page for its dates.

## For international students

Two official rules relate specifically to advanced degrees from US institutions. Confirm how they apply to you with your DSO and the employer's immigration team; this is not legal advice.

::: evidence official
USCIS says the H-1B program has an annual cap of 65,000, plus 20,000 additional petitions for beneficiaries who have earned a master's degree or higher from a U.S. institution of higher education ([USCIS H-1B](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/h-1b-specialty-occupations)). USCIS also says a student who later earns a new qualifying STEM degree at a higher level may be eligible for one additional STEM OPT extension ([USCIS STEM OPT](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/students-and-exchange-visitors/optional-practical-training-extension-for-stem-students-stem-opt)).
:::

These rules affect eligibility, not whether a particular employer sponsors. Read [OPT and STEM OPT](../opt-and-stem-opt/) and [Cap-exempt H-1B and other visas](../cap-exempt-h1b-and-other-visas/), then ask each employer directly.

## Pay

Do not assume a fixed premium for an advanced degree. Some employers pay advanced-degree hires differently and some do not; the posting's range and the recruiter's answer about level are your evidence. [How to research your market salary](../compensation-research/) explains how to build a range for your role, level and location.
