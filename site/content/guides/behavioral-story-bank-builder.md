The standard advice — "prepare six STAR stories" — leaves out the multiplier. A well-prepared story may fit several question families, but no fixed number of stories covers every interview. Memorising fifty answers covers it less well, because the answers drift from each other and the interviewer can tell.

This guide is the worksheet: build ten stories, each with the structure that turns it into three or four answers. Each story uses STAR with a fifth step — reflection — that separates a good answer from a rehearsed one. The STAR-R structure is the same one [Behavioral interviews](../behavioral-interviews/) describes; this guide is the bank.

::: evidence practical
One strong story answered in three different framings beats three weak stories told separately. Interviewers re-use questions; the bank makes every story reusable.
:::

## Why a bank, not a list

Two reasons.

- You will forget the details of a story you have not used in a month. The bank forces you to keep them sharp.
- Interviewers re-use question types. The "tell me about a conflict" question at company A and the "describe a difficult teammate" question at company B are the same story. Adapt the emphasis only when the example actually answers the new question; keep the underlying facts consistent.

## The bank

Start with ten stories. Each row is a story; the columns are the question families it can answer.

| Story | Skill demonstrated | Question families it answers |
|---|---|---|
| Biggest failure | Ownership | Failure, learning, ownership |
| Conflict with a teammate | Communication | Conflict, teamwork, feedback |
| A difficult project | Problem solving | Ambiguity, problem solving, technical depth |
| A leadership moment | Leadership | Leadership, ownership, motivation |
| An ambiguous problem | Judgment | Ambiguity, judgment, prioritisation |
| A technical challenge | Technical depth | Technical depth, debugging, learning |
| A deadline crunch | Execution | Execution, prioritisation, ownership |
| Feedback I received | Growth | Feedback, growth, learning |
| A customer or user problem | Customer focus | Customer focus, problem solving, judgment |
| An ethical decision | Integrity | Integrity, judgment, ownership |

The exact stories are yours. The skill column is what makes the story reusable; the question families are what you can map to it.

## Each story, fully prepared

For each story, write down five things.

### Situation

One or two sentences of context. Names, places, dates are good. Specific beats impressive.

- _Example:_ "In my spring semester, I was leading a four-person team for the senior design project. We had eight weeks, and our original project lead disappeared in week three."

### Task

What you had to do, and what made it hard.

- _Example:_ "I had to either rebuild the team plan or pull the project. The deadline was fixed; the team was down a member; I had no experience leading."

### Action

What you, specifically, did. Most of the answer is here. Use "I", not "we", for the actions you took.

- _Example:_ "I scheduled a 30-minute sync with the original lead to recover what I could of their work. I redistributed the four remaining tasks based on each person's strengths. I cut the project scope by one feature."

### Result

What happened, with an honest number or outcome.

- _Example:_ "We shipped on time. The cut feature was the lowest-priority one anyway. The team gave me the highest peer-eval score I'd received in the program."

### Reflection

What you would do differently. This is the fifth step and the one most candidates skip.

- _Example:_ "I should have flagged the risk to the team earlier. I waited until week five to raise the missing-member concern; I should have raised it when the lead became unavailable in week three. Now I ask 'what's the worst case here?' before I start."

The reflection is what makes the answer honest. Without it, the answer is a victory lap; with it, the answer shows learning.

## How to use the bank

### Mapping

For each story, list the three or four behavioral questions it can answer. Here is the mapping from the table above.

- **Biggest failure** answers: "Tell me about a time you failed.", "Tell me about a time you had to ask for help.", "Tell me about a time you recovered from a setback."
- **Conflict** answers: "Tell me about a time you disagreed with a teammate.", "Tell me about a time you had to work with someone difficult.", "Describe a time you gave someone difficult feedback."
- **Difficult project** answers: "Tell me about the hardest project you've worked on.", "Describe a complex problem you solved.", "Tell me about a time you had to learn something new quickly."
- **Leadership** answers: "Tell me about a time you led a team.", "Describe a time you influenced without authority.", "Tell me about a time you motivated others."
- **Ambiguous problem** answers: "Tell me about a time you had to make a decision with incomplete information.", "Describe a time you prioritised competing demands.", "Tell me about a time you defined the problem yourself."
- **Technical challenge** answers: "Tell me about the most difficult technical problem you solved.", "Describe a time you had to debug something hard.", "Tell me about a time you made a trade-off."
- **Deadline crunch** answers: "Tell me about a time you had to ship under pressure.", "Describe a time you managed multiple competing deadlines.", "Tell me about a time you cut scope to hit a deadline."
- **Feedback** answers: "Tell me about a time you received critical feedback.", "Describe a time you changed your approach based on input.", "Tell me about a time you helped someone else grow."
- **Customer / user problem** answers: "Tell me about a time you solved a user problem.", "Describe a time you had to make a trade-off between user needs and engineering constraints.", "Tell me about a time you changed a product based on feedback."
- **Ethical decision** answers: "Tell me about a time you faced an ethical dilemma.", "Describe a time you pushed back on something you disagreed with.", "Tell me about a time you prioritised the right thing over the easy thing."

### Framing

Each story has a default framing — the one it was designed for. For other framings, change the lead sentence.

- A failure story framed for "ownership" leads with "I owned the outcome and here is what I did."
- The same failure story framed for "learning" leads with "I learned X and here is what I changed afterwards."
- The same failure story framed for "feedback" leads with "I received feedback that changed my approach."

The story content does not change. The lead sentence does.

### Length

Answer in two minutes or less. Interviewers ask follow-ups; that is the interview working. Practice with a timer.

## How to find your stories

If you do not yet have ten stories, the easiest source is your own history.

- One from a group project (teamwork, conflict, leadership).
- One from a job or internship (execution, ownership, customer focus).
- One from coursework (technical depth, ambiguous problem).
- One from a personal project (ownership, execution).
- One from an extracurricular (leadership, integrity, customer focus).

If you do not have ten distinct examples, start with fewer. Use real coursework, volunteering or part-time work and add examples over time. Never manufacture a story to fill a slot.

## What if you have not had a job?

The bank is built from any source. A retail job, a part-time job, a class project, a club, a side project, a hackathon, a volunteer role. The story does not need to be technical to answer a technical interviewer's question; the skill does.

## Practice

- **Out loud.** The bank is useless until you have said it.
- **With a timer.** Check whether your answer fits the available time while covering your actions and outcome.
- **With a peer or a model.** A model can run a behavioral round; a peer can give you the social cues. See [The AI Job Search Framework](../ai-job-search-framework/) section D.
- **On the bank, not on the questions.** Practice the bank by mapping it to random questions; the questions will vary, the bank will not.

## When the round is recorded

Some companies run the behavioral round as an AI-scored or AI-moderated recorded interview. The same bank works; the pacing is different. See [AI-assisted interviews](../ai-assisted-interviews/) for the format-specific prep.
