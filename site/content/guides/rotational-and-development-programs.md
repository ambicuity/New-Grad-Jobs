Employers in many fields hire graduates into structured programs rather than single roles. Common examples include finance leadership programs, engineering development programs, operations and supply-chain rotations, sales training programs, HR and IT rotations, and healthcare administrative fellowships. This guide explains how these programs differ from a direct hire, using what three employers publish about their own programs. Each program sets its own rules, so the details below describe those programs only.

## How a program differs from a direct hire

A direct-hire posting fills one role on one team. A program hires a cohort and moves participants through a planned sequence of assignments. Programs usually share these features:

- **Rotations.** You work in two or more assignments, sometimes in different locations or business units.
- **Structured training.** The employer adds courses, cohort events or sponsored certifications to the day job.
- **A cohort and a start date.** Participants often start together, so applications open and close on the program's schedule, not when a single team needs someone.
- **A placement at the end.** Some programs say what happens after the last rotation, and some do not. Ask.

Neither path is better in general. A program gives breadth and structure. A direct hire lets you go deep on one team from the start and usually leaves the location up to you and the team. The right choice depends on what you want to learn and how much you can move.

Some employers also run programs in operations, sales, HR and IT. Look for program titles on their careers pages, or search for the titles on the board.

## When programs recruit

Program calendars are set by each employer, and many publish no fixed date. J&J says its [program application windows vary](https://www.careers.jnj.com/finance-leadership-development-program) and offers job alerts. Penn State Health publishes a closing date on its fellowship page (30 September for the 2026 cycle). Some programs recruit well before the start date. For example, J&J posted a [2027 FLDP role](https://www.careers.jnj.com/en/jobs/r-093407/2027-finance-leadership-development-program-fldp-full-time-el-paso-tx-juarez-mx/) on 24 August 2026 for degrees earned between December 2026 and June 2027, so a program can open close to a year before it starts. Practical steps:

1. List the programs you would accept, and note each one's published dates, or the absence of dates.
2. Set alerts on the employer's careers site and on the board. See [How to search for new grad jobs](../how-to-search-for-new-grad-jobs/).
3. Check whether the program hires from internships first. J&J's program pages list summer internships alongside full-time roles.
4. Read [The new grad hiring timeline](../new-grad-hiring-timeline/) for the wider calendar, which varies by field and country.

## Questions to ask before you apply or accept

Programs publish what they choose to publish. The questions below cover the gaps. Recruiters and current participants can answer most of them.

1. **Placement.** What happens after the final rotation? Is a permanent role guaranteed, and who decides where I go?
2. **Rotation choice.** Do I choose rotations, rank preferences, or get assigned?
3. **Location.** Will I need to relocate between rotations? Is relocation paid?
4. **Level and pay.** Do pay and title change between rotations or at graduation from the program?
5. **Training commitments.** If the employer pays for a degree or certification, is there a service or repayment clause? Ask for the wording. [Staffing agencies and contract roles](../staffing-agencies-and-contract-roles/) explains what training repayment terms look like.
6. **Eligibility.** Are there graduation-date windows, degree or GPA requirements, or work-authorization limits? Some program postings state them. See [Visa sponsorship and citizenship flags](../visa-sponsorship-and-citizenship/).
7. **Leaving early.** What happens if a rotation is a poor fit? Can I move to a direct role?

If a program's page and the recruiter disagree, ask for the written terms. Your offer letter is what governs.

## Finding programs on the board

The board's title rules treat phrases such as "rotational program", "graduate program", "development program" and "analyst program" as entry-level signals, so these roles can appear without "new grad" in the title. To find them:

- Type `rotational`, `development program` or `graduate program` into the search box. Every word you type must appear in the title, company or location.
- A program title with none of those phrases, such as many administrative fellowships, may not reach the main list, and titles with the whole word "Fellow" are excluded outright. Search for them in the EXPLORE tab, described in [Finding hidden new grad roles](../finding-hidden-new-grad-roles/).
- Program titles can fall under different ROLE chips, so leave ROLE empty for this search. [How NGJ classifies jobs](../how-ngj-classifies-jobs/) explains how categories are chosen.
- Use the [browse pages](../../jobs/) to check a target employer's open roles.

A posting that mentions a program is still an employer's description, so read it in full. [What counts as a new grad job?](../what-counts-as-a-new-grad-job/) explains what the board's rules can and cannot judge.

## A quick comparison worksheet

For each program and direct-hire option you are weighing, write down:

- the length, the number of rotations, and whether you choose them
- the locations and relocation terms
- the training offered and any repayment clause
- the placement after the program, and whether it is guaranteed in writing
- the pay at the start and after the program
- your own goal: breadth across a function, or depth on one team

Compare offers with [Comparing offers](../comparing-offers/) once you have them.
