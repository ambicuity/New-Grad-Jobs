The templates below are starting points. Replace the brackets with your specifics; cut what does not apply. Short emails are read; long emails are skimmed. Aim for five sentences or fewer unless the situation calls for more.

::: evidence practical
Send the email within 24 hours of the event. Memory fades; relevance fades faster. The recruiter who hears from you within a day is a different recruiter than the one who hears from you in a week.
:::

## Thank-you after an interview

The classic. Send to every interviewer, not just the recruiter.

> **Subject:** Thanks for your time today
>
> Hi [name],
>
> Thanks for the conversation about [role] today. I enjoyed learning more about [one specific thing — a project, a team decision, a question they asked].
>
> I'm still excited about the role. Please let me know if there's anything else I can share that would help.
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

Why it works: short, specific, asks nothing. The specificity (one thing you learned) is what makes it memorable.

## Thank-you after an AI-scored interview

The same structure works. The platform records your response, not your follow-up, so the email is to the recruiter.

> **Subject:** Thanks for the interview
>
> Hi [recruiter name],
>
> Thanks for the opportunity to interview for [role]. I appreciated the chance to think through [one specific question] in particular.
>
> Looking forward to hearing what's next.
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

## Recruiter follow-up when you have not heard back

Send after one week of silence. If still no response after a second week, follow up once more; after that, redirect your energy.

> **Subject:** Following up on [role] application
>
> Hi [recruiter name],
>
> I wanted to follow up on my application for [role] from [date]. I'm still interested and would love to know if there's any additional information I can provide.
>
> If the timing isn't right, I understand — happy to reconnect later if appropriate.
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

## Post-interview follow-up (proactive)

When you want to add information you did not get to in the interview.

> **Subject:** Quick follow-up on [role]
>
> Hi [interviewer name],
>
> Thanks again for the conversation. I wanted to share [one sentence — a portfolio piece, a project you forgot to mention, an answer you re-thought]. The link is [URL].
>
> Still excited about the role. Let me know if there's anything else I can help with.
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

## Feedback request after a rejection

Most recruiters will not give detailed feedback, but asking once is appropriate. Send within a week of the rejection email.

> **Subject:** Quick ask — feedback on [role] interview
>
> Hi [recruiter name],
>
> Thanks for letting me know about the [role] decision. I enjoyed the process and would value any feedback you could share — even a sentence or two on what would have made my application stronger.
>
> I won't take a lot of your time. Thanks for considering it.
>
> Best,
> [Your name]

If they do not respond in a week, send a brief thank-you that closes the loop.

## Response to a rejection (closing the loop)

Optional. Useful when you want to leave the door open.

> **Subject:** Thanks for the update
>
> Hi [recruiter name],
>
> Thanks for the update on [role]. I appreciated the opportunity and the conversation with the team.
>
> I'd love to be considered for future roles that fit, particularly [one role family or team]. If something comes up, please keep me in mind.
>
> Best,
> [Your name]

## Referral request (cold)

Use after building a small connection. Not the first email.

> **Subject:** Quick question — referrals at [Company]
>
> Hi [name],
>
> I'm in the middle of a new-grad search for [role family] and noticed [Company] has [one specific role] open. If you're comfortable putting in a referral, I'd be grateful.
>
> I've attached my résumé and a one-paragraph summary of why I'm interested. Happy to share anything else that helps.
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

See [How to ask for a referral](../how-to-ask-for-a-referral/) for the longer version.

## Recruiter re-engagement (months later)

For when you want to reopen a conversation that went cold.

> **Subject:** Checking back in
>
> Hi [recruiter name],
>
> We spoke back in [month] about [role]. I wanted to check back in — I'm still in the search for [role family] and would love to know what's open at [Company] now.
>
> My situation has changed in [one sentence — new project, new graduation date, etc.] and I think the fit might be closer than it was.
>
> Thanks for considering,
> [Your name]

## Application follow-up on a posting that has gone silent

Use after two weeks of silence on an unsolicited submission.

> **Subject:** Following up on [role] application
>
> Hi [recruiter or hiring manager name],
>
> I applied for [role] on [date] and wanted to follow up. I'm still very interested in the role and the team.
>
> If the timing isn't right, I understand. If there's any additional information I can provide, I'm happy to share.
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

## Offer clarification request

Use when an offer is on the table and you have specific questions.

> **Subject:** Questions about [Company] offer
>
> Hi [recruiter name],
>
> Thanks for the offer — I'm excited about the role and want to make sure I understand the package. A few questions:
>
> 1. [Question about base, bonus, equity]
> 2. [Question about start date or relocation]
> 3. [Question about benefits or visa]
>
> Happy to discuss by phone or email.
>
> Thanks,
> [Your name]

See [Evaluating an offer](../evaluating-an-offer/) and [Negotiation](../negotiation/) for the substantive questions.

## What the templates leave out

The templates are intentionally generic. Add the specifics that make them yours.

- A specific project you worked on.
- A specific person on the team you admire.
- A specific question from the interview you are still thinking about.

The specificity is what turns a template into a real email.

## What to never write

- A long complaint about the rejection.
- A demand for feedback ("I deserve to know why").
- A counter-offer by email (negotiation belongs in a call).
- A copy-paste that names the wrong company.

If you find yourself writing any of these, sleep on it and rewrite.
