Postings talk about work authorization in a small number of stock phrases, and the phrases are not all equivalent. Knowing them saves you from applying to roles you cannot take and from skipping roles you could.

## Phrases that mean no sponsorship

- "We are unable to sponsor" / "cannot sponsor" / "will not sponsor" a work visa, now or in the future
- "No sponsorship available for this position"
- "Must be authorized to work in the US without sponsorship"

These are a policy for the role. The board sets its **no sponsorship** flag when it reads phrases like these, and the "no restriction stated" chip hides the posting.

## Phrases that sound stricter than they are

- **"Must be authorized to work in the United States."** On its own, without "without sponsorship", this is true of every legal hire and is often boilerplate. A student with valid OPT authorization may be authorized for the role. The board may flag explicit authorization language conservatively; that automated flag does not establish a no-sponsorship policy, so check the full sentence.
- **"Legally eligible to work in Canada"** or the Indian equivalents follow the same pattern.
- **"Sponsorship considered on a case-by-case basis."** Not a no. Ask.
- **"Must be able to obtain a work permit."** Depends on the country and on you.

## Citizenship, clearance and export-control phrases

- "US citizenship required" / "must be a US citizen"
- "Ability to obtain and maintain a security clearance", "active clearance required", "Secret", "Top Secret", "TS/SCI"
- References to export control or ITAR may involve a U.S.-person requirement, which can include noncitizens

The board uses a broad **citizenship required** text flag for these signals. It does not distinguish every legal category. Export controls do not require citizens-only hiring; [DOJ explains the distinction](https://www.justice.gov/crt/iers-frequently-asked-questions-faqs). [Citizenship and clearance](../citizenship-and-clearance/) explains these requirements.

## Silence

Most postings say nothing at all. The board shows them as "no restriction stated", which is a description of the text, not a promise from the employer. Ask about the policy in the first recruiter conversation, regardless of employer size. [OPT and STEM OPT](../opt-and-stem-opt/) lists the questions.

## How the flags are set

The scraper looks for these phrases in the title and description as whole phrases, not substrings, so "sponsorship" inside "our sponsorship program for conferences" does not trip it, but a posting that phrases its policy unusually may go unflagged. Flags can have false positives and false negatives. Always read the original posting. The exact phrase lists are in the scraper's source on [GitHub](https://github.com/ambicuity/New-Grad-Jobs).

## What to do with a flagged posting

If you need future sponsorship, ask whether the employer accepts your current authorization and later needs. A no-sponsorship policy may exclude OPT candidates. Apply only after you understand the policy; answer application questions truthfully and make a plan with your DSO for authorization deadlines.
