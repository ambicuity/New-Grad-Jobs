This guide is for F-1 students on post-completion OPT or the STEM extension who are between jobs, still searching, or about to change employers. The core idea: during post-completion OPT your status depends on employment, the regulation caps how many days you can be unemployed, and the safest habit is to report on time and keep your own evidence. For the basics of OPT itself, read [OPT and STEM OPT](../opt-and-stem-opt/) and the [international student job search center](../international-student-job-search-center/) first. Confirm anything here that affects you with your Designated School Official (DSO).

## The limits, as the regulation states them

::: evidence official
[8 CFR 214.2(f)(10)(ii)(E)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/subpart-A/section-214.2) says: "During post-completion OPT, F-1 status is dependent upon employment. Students may not accrue an aggregate of more than 90 days of unemployment during any post-completion OPT period." Students granted the 24-month STEM extension "may not accrue an aggregate of more than 150 days of unemployment during a total OPT period," which includes the initial post-completion period.
:::

Two things follow from that wording. First, the limit is an aggregate: separate gaps add up. Second, the 150-day figure covers the whole OPT period, so days used during initial OPT count toward it. It is not 150 new days.

The DSO-facing [Study in the States OPT page](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/sevis-help-hub/student-records/fm-student-employment/f-1-optional-practical-training-opt) adds that "a student whose record lacks employer information is considered unemployed," and that SEVP officials can terminate a record that reaches 90 days of unemployment. In other words, the count SEVP sees depends on what is in your SEVIS record, not only on whether you were working. If you were working but nobody reported it, the record can still show you as unemployed.

SEVP's [2010 OPT policy guidance](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/sevis/pdf/opt_policy_guidance_042010.pdf) counts every day without qualifying work, weekends included, from the start date on your EAD. Questions the regulation does not answer for your case, such as the date your count started, how a particular gap is counted, or what happens near the limit, belong with your DSO. DHS's [fixed-period admission final rule](https://www.federalregister.gov/documents/2026/07/17/2026-14439/establishing-a-fixed-time-period-of-admission-and-an-extension-of-stay-procedure-for-nonimmigrant) states an effective date of September 15, 2026; the [international student center](../international-student-job-search-center/) explains why you should have your DSO confirm which rules apply to your admission.

## What DHS says can count as employment

The most detailed list is SEVP's [Policy Guidance 1004-03](https://www.ice.gov/doclib/sevis/pdf/opt_policy_guidance_042010.pdf) from April 2010. ICE's [practical training page](https://www.ice.gov/sevis/practical-training) says that guidance remains in effect, except that the later 24-month STEM rule governs where the two conflict. For regular post-completion OPT, the guidance says all employment must be related to your degree program, and it lists:

- **Paid employment**, part time (at least 20 hours per week on post-completion OPT) or full time.
- **Multiple employers**, as long as every job is related to your degree.
- **Short-term multiple employers**, for example performing artists doing gigs. Keep a list of every gig with dates and duration.
- **Work for hire (1099 work).** Be ready to show the contract period and the contracting company's name and address if DHS asks.
- **Self-employment.** You must be able to show proper business licences and that you are actively running a business related to your degree.
- **Agency or consulting-firm employment.** You must be able to show you averaged at least 20 hours per week.
- **Unpaid work.** The guidance says a student "may work as a volunteer or unpaid intern, where this practice does not violate any labor laws." The work must be at least 20 hours per week, and you must be able to show evidence from the employer that you did.

STEM OPT is stricter. A Study in the States post on [volunteer positions](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/2018/07/reporting-volunteer-positions-during-opt-employment) (July 2018, which the page notes may be outdated) says volunteer positions do not meet the conditions of a STEM extension, that STEM students "must receive compensation," and that a volunteer position not directly related to the course of study should not be reported. It adds that such positions "do not stop the accrual of unemployment." The regulation also says STEM training may not be less than 20 hours per week. Each STEM employer must be enrolled in E-Verify and sign a Form I-983; see [OPT and STEM OPT](../opt-and-stem-opt/).

Unpaid work raises a separate question: whether the arrangement is lawful under US wage law. The SEVP guidance makes that a condition, so do not treat an unpaid role as a way to stop the clock without asking your DSO first.

## Report on time

::: evidence official
Under [8 CFR 214.2(f)(12)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/subpart-A/section-214.2), a student on OPT must report any change of name or address, or interruption of employment, to the DSO. STEM extension students must report a change of legal name, address, employer name, employer address or loss of employment within 10 days, and complete a validation report every six months.
:::

How you report:

1. **Post-completion OPT.** The [SEVP Portal](https://studyinthestates.dhs.gov/sevp-portal-help/getting-started/sevp-portal-overview) lets you update your addresses, phone number and employer information yourself. SEVP's 2010 guidance recommends reporting any employment change within 10 business days. Your school may also ask for its own form.
2. **STEM OPT.** The SEVP Portal page says STEM students cannot add a new employer or change an employer's start date there. Your DSO enters it, and a new employer needs a new Form I-983. The regulation gives 10 days from starting with the new employer to submit it.
3. **Leaving the US early.** The 2010 guidance includes reporting that you are completing OPT and exiting the United States, with the exit date.

## Keep your own records

SEVP's 2010 guidance recommends keeping evidence for each job and keeping a record of every report you send your DSO. A simple folder, kept for the whole OPT period, can hold:

| Record | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Offer letter and each job description | Shows the job title, duties and start date |
| Supervisor's name and contact details | The guidance lists supervisor contact information as evidence to keep |
| Letter linking duties to your degree | The guidance suggests one when the job description does not make the link clear |
| Pay stubs or a timesheet | Shows hours, especially for part-time, agency or unpaid work |
| Contracts, invoices and gig list | Shows dates and the contracting company for 1099 work |
| Business licences | Needed if you are self-employed |
| Screenshots or emails of each report | Shows what you reported and when |
| Your own running count of gaps | Lets you compare against what your DSO sees in SEVIS |

## While you are still searching

- Ask your DSO what your SEVIS record currently shows, and keep a written note of the answer.
- Use the board to find roles: start with [new this week](../../jobs/new-this-week/) and [no visa restriction stated](../../jobs/no-visa-restriction/), and read [Sponsorship language](../sponsorship-language/) before you apply.
- If an offer you were counting on falls through, report the change and see [Rescinded offers and reneging](../rescinded-offers-and-reneging/).
- If you are close to the limit, talk to your DSO before you accept a role you are unsure qualifies.

NGJ reads employers' own career sites plus Indeed searches, and drops postings whose titles mark them as part-time, seasonal, temporary, fixed-term or contract. Indeed results are not filtered by employer type, and a posting that does not say so in its title can still appear. It cannot tell you whether a job is related to your degree. Confirm that with your DSO.
