The board covers the United States, Canada and India, but the three hiring systems differ. This guide is for graduates searching in Canada or India, including international graduates. It explains how campus hiring tends to run in each country, where work-authorization rules are published, and how to set up the board for either country. Rules on permits and employment change, so treat everything here as a starting point and confirm it with the official source.

## How graduate hiring works in Canada

Many Canadian universities and colleges run co-operative education, where study terms alternate with paid work terms. The University of Waterloo, for example, says its co-op students [typically alternate four months in school with four months as a full-time employee](https://uwaterloo.ca/future-students/co-op) and complete four to six work terms. Programs at other institutions are structured differently, so check your own.

What this means for a new grad search:

- **Co-op employers can be an early route to an offer.** If you had a good work term, ask your manager whether the team hires graduates and when.
- **Co-op and graduate roles are separate postings.** The board treats co-ops like internships and keeps them out of the main list. Turn on the internships & co-ops toggle described in [Finding hidden new grad roles](../finding-hidden-new-grad-roles/) only if you still have work terms to complete.
- **Graduate titles vary.** Canadian postings use "new graduate", "graduate program", "junior", "associate" and "Engineer I". Search for each, and read [Reading a new grad posting](../reading-a-new-grad-posting/) for what the level markers mean.

Start with your school's career centre for employer events and job boards. Then add direct applications to employers on your [target-company list](../build-a-target-company-list/).

## Work authorization for international graduates in Canada

Graduating in Canada does not authorize you to work. IRCC describes two broad kinds of work permit: [an employer-specific permit, for which you need a job offer, and an open permit, for which you do not](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/work-canada/need-permit.html). An employer-specific permit may require the employer to get a labour market impact assessment (LMIA).

Many graduates look at the Post-Graduation Work Permit (PGWP).

::: evidence official
IRCC says graduating from a designated learning institution [does not automatically make you eligible for a PGWP](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/after-graduation/about.html), and you can get only one PGWP. Its [eligibility page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/after-graduation/eligibility.html) says you must apply within 180 days of confirmation that you completed your program, and you must meet a language requirement, with limited exceptions. Some college and non-degree programs must also be in an eligible field of study; bachelor's, master's and doctoral degrees are exempt from that rule.
:::

The permit's length depends on your program and is limited by your passport's validity. Before you accept a start date, read IRCC's [application page](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/work/after-graduation/apply.html) on when you can and cannot work while an application is processed. Do not assume you can start. Confirm your own case with IRCC's pages, your school's international student advisors and, where needed, a licensed immigration consultant or lawyer. [Visa sponsorship and citizenship flags](../visa-sponsorship-and-citizenship/) explains what the board's visa chips can and cannot tell you.

## How graduate hiring works in India

At many Indian institutes, the placement cell runs campus placement. Employers register, students apply through the cell, and the cell sets the rules. These rules differ by institute. For example, IIT (BHU) Varanasi's [student placement policy](https://placement.iitbhu.ac.in/policies/students) describes a One-Student-One-Job policy, with exceptions. It counts an accepted pre-placement offer (PPO) as a job and bars a student who accepts one from further campus placement. Read your own institute's policy before you accept an offer.

Off-campus hiring runs alongside campus placement:

- **Employer career sites.** Many employers post graduate and "freshers" roles on their own sites, and the board reads those sites.
- **Off-campus or pool drives.** Some employers run one hiring event for students from several colleges. Confirm a drive through the employer's own careers page or your placement cell before you share documents.
- **Government job services.** The [National Career Service](https://www.ncs.gov.in/), run by the Ministry of Labour & Employment, lists vacancies and job fairs. It says its services are free and that jobseekers should not pay fees to employers.

If someone asks you to pay for a drive, a test or an offer letter, stop and read [How to detect a fake job or recruiter](../how-to-detect-a-fake-job-or-recruiter/).

Foreign nationals who want to work in India need Indian authorization. A US or Canadian permit does not cover India. The Indian sources are linked in [Visa sponsorship and citizenship flags](../visa-sponsorship-and-citizenship/). Confirm current requirements with the Indian mission that handles your application.

## Offer letters, joining dates and notice periods

Get your terms in writing in both countries, and ask about anything the letter leaves out. Notice periods and probation terms come from your offer or appointment letter and the law that applies where you work, so ask HR rather than guessing.

1. Is this a permanent, full-time role? What is the job title and level?
2. What is the joining date, and what happens if my graduation, permit or documents are delayed?
3. Is there a probation period, and what notice applies during and after it?
4. For India, what does the CTC include, and what is the fixed monthly gross? See [How to research your market salary](../compensation-research/).
5. Is there a service agreement, bond, or repayment clause for training or relocation? Ask for the exact wording before you sign.
6. Which documents and background checks are needed, and when? See [Background checks and pre-employment verification](../background-checks-and-pre-employment-verification/).

If you hold more than one offer, read your placement cell's rules and [Questions to ask before accepting](../questions-before-accepting/) before you commit.

## Set up the board for Canada or India

1. Pick your ROLE, then choose **canada** or **india** under COUNTRY. You can select both.
2. The LOCATION facet lists US and Canadian metros only. For Indian cities, and any city not listed, type it into the search box, for example `Toronto`, `Bengaluru` or `Pune`. Search matches company, title and location.
3. Bookmark the URL. Your filters are saved in it.
4. If you need sponsorship in Canada, the "no restriction stated" chip hides postings whose text matches NGJ's sponsorship and citizenship phrases, which are written mainly for US postings. Canadian wording such as "legally eligible to work in Canada" may not be caught, so read the posting. A posting that states no restriction is not making a promise, so confirm with the employer.
5. Follow the routine in [How to search for new grad jobs](../how-to-search-for-new-grad-jobs/), and check the [browse pages](../../jobs/) for company pages.

The board shows only postings that employers put on the career sites it reads, or that appear in its Indeed searches. Some campus-placement roles are never posted publicly, so keep your placement cell or career centre in the loop.

## Rules change

Work-permit rules, language requirements and placement policies change, sometimes in the middle of a hiring season. Every rule in this guide links to its source. Before you act, re-read that source and confirm your situation with IRCC, your institute or an official adviser. This guide is general information, not legal or immigration advice.
