A hundred applications with no interviews is a reason to review your search. The record can help you identify controllable improvements, but it does not by itself establish why employers did not respond.

## Step 1: confirm the résumé is being read

If you have applied to fifty or more postings on the board and have heard nothing, review the search and résumé together; silence alone does not locate the problem. Run [An ATS-friendly résumé](../ats-friendly-resume/) and [Resume bullet points](../resume-bullet-points/) against what you sent. Two questions:

- Are you getting recruiter screens at companies you have applied to through the careers portal (not just the board)? If yes, the board postings are not the only signal.
- Are you getting recruiter views on LinkedIn? A profile view shows interest in that profile, not that anyone read a particular application résumé.

## Step 2: confirm you are applying to roles that fit

A hundred applications is not a hundred real shots if most of them are filtered out by the ATS before a person sees them. Audit the last twenty:

- How many asked for a graduation date outside your window?
- How many required a clearance or citizenship you do not have?
- How many were for "2+ years experience" roles that slipped past the board's filter?

If many applications failed stated eligibility, review how you select roles. Tighten it: see [How to build a target-company list](../build-a-target-company-list/) and [How to search for new grad jobs](../how-to-search-for-new-grad-jobs/).

## Step 3: confirm you are applying early

Check whether your target employers review on a rolling basis or use fixed deadlines. A posting more than a week old can still be open and actively reviewed. The board marks "first seen in the last 24 hours" for a reason. See [Application strategy](../application-strategy/) for timing and tracking.

## Step 4: confirm the résumé matches the role

Review whether the résumé makes your relevant experience clear; silence alone does not prove a mismatch. Pick the three applications you most wanted and compare your résumé line-by-line against the posting. Specifically:

- Does your top bullet use the same words the posting uses for the role's main skill?
- Does your projects section include a project of the same shape as the role?
- Does your degree and graduation date appear at the top, where it is easy to find?

A missing piece of relevant evidence is worth improving, but none of these checks identifies the cause of rejection by itself.

## Step 5: get external eyes

After you have done the audit, show your résumé to two people who have hired for the role you want. Ask them to read it for ten seconds and tell you what they remember. The gap between what you meant and what they read is the next thing to fix.

## When it is not the résumé

If the résumé is strong, the targets are tight, the timing is early, and you still get nothing, the next thing to try is changing the channel. Get a referral at a target company ([How to ask for a referral](../how-to-ask-for-a-referral/)) or message a recruiter directly ([How to message recruiters as a new grad](../messaging-recruiters/)). Referral handling varies; it can provide context but does not guarantee priority or an interview.

If you have done all of this and the funnel is still silent after another two weeks, read [Diagnose your job-search funnel](../diagnose-your-job-search-funnel/) for a fuller model of what is breaking and where.
