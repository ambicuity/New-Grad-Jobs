A hundred applications with no interviews is a problem. It is also data: the problem is somewhere specific, and you can find it without sending another application.

## Step 1: confirm the résumé is being read

If you have applied to fifty or more postings on the board and have heard nothing, the first suspect is the résumé, not the search. Run [An ATS-friendly résumé](../ats-friendly-resume/) and [Resume bullet points](../resume-bullet-points/) against what you sent. Two questions:

- Are you getting recruiter screens at companies you have applied to through the careers portal (not just the board)? If yes, the board postings are not the only signal.
- Are you getting recruiter views on LinkedIn? If yes, the résumé is being read; the issue is the next step.

## Step 2: confirm you are applying to roles that fit

A hundred applications is not a hundred real shots if most of them are filtered out by the ATS before a person sees them. Audit the last twenty:

- How many asked for a graduation date outside your window?
- How many required a clearance or citizenship you do not have?
- How many were for "2+ years experience" roles that slipped past the board's filter?

If a quarter or more of your applications were outside your profile, the funnel is too wide. Tighten it: see [How to build a target-company list](../build-a-target-company-list/) and [How to search for new grad jobs](../how-to-search-for-new-grad-jobs/).

## Step 3: confirm you are applying early

If most of your applications are more than a week after the posting appeared, you are applying into closing requisitions. The board marks "first seen in the last 24 hours" for a reason. See [Application strategy](../application-strategy/) for the timing math.

## Step 4: confirm the résumé matches the role

If the résumé is being read but no recruiter is responding, the match is the problem. Pick the three applications you most wanted and compare your résumé line-by-line against the posting. Specifically:

- Does your top bullet use the same words the posting uses for the role's main skill?
- Does your projects section include a project of the same shape as the role?
- Does your degree and graduation date appear at the top, where a recruiter will see it in five seconds?

If the answer to any of those is "no," that is the fix.

## Step 5: get external eyes

After you have done the audit, show your résumé to two people who have hired for the role you want. Ask them to read it for ten seconds and tell you what they remember. The gap between what you meant and what they read is the next thing to fix.

## When it is not the résumé

If the résumé is strong, the targets are tight, the timing is early, and you still get nothing, the next thing to try is changing the channel. Get a referral at a target company ([How to ask for a referral](../how-to-ask-for-a-referral/)) or message a recruiter directly ([How to message recruiters as a new grad](../messaging-recruiters/)). Referrals skip the line; one or two can change the pattern.

If you have done all of this and the funnel is still silent after another two weeks, read [Diagnose your job-search funnel](../diagnose-your-job-search-funnel/) for a fuller model of what is breaking and where.
