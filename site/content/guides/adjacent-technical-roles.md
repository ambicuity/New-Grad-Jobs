Many graduates search for one title, such as software engineer, mechanical engineer or financial analyst, and miss the roles beside it. Those roles use the same skills and often hire at entry level. This guide describes several of them, links the US Bureau of Labor Statistics (BLS) occupation profile where one exists, and shows how to judge and find them. Job titles vary by employer, so always read the duties in the posting rather than relying on the title.

## The roles

| Role | What the work involves | Official profile |
| --- | --- | --- |
| QA or test engineer | Designing and running tests, writing test plans, and reporting defects to developers | [Software developers, quality assurance analysts and testers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/software-developers.htm) |
| Sales or solutions engineer | Selling products or services that need technical expertise, often by explaining and demonstrating them | [Sales engineers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/sales/sales-engineers.htm) |
| Technical support | Helping users fix problems (user support), or maintaining and troubleshooting networks (network support) | [Computer support specialists](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/computer-support-specialists.htm) |
| IT or systems administration | Installing, configuring and maintaining an organization's networks and systems | [Network and computer systems administrators](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/computer-and-information-technology/network-and-computer-systems-administrators.htm) |
| Data or business analyst | Collecting and analysing data and reporting what it shows | [Operations research analysts](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/operations-research-analysts.htm), [market research analysts](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/business-and-financial/market-research-analysts.htm) and [data scientists](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/math/data-scientists.htm) cover overlapping work |
| Technical writer | Writing manuals, how-to guides and other documents that make technical material easier to follow | [Technical writers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/technical-writers.htm) |
| Quality, manufacturing or process engineer | Improving production systems, quality control and the processes that make a product | [Industrial engineers](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/architecture-and-engineering/industrial-engineers.htm), which covers manufacturing and validation engineers |
| Implementation or customer success | Setting up a product for a customer, training their staff, and staying their technical contact | No single BLS profile; read the posting's duties |
| Field engineer | Installing, commissioning or servicing equipment at customer or project sites, often with travel | No single BLS profile; duties vary widely by industry |

::: evidence official
BLS says newly hired sales engineers [typically need on-the-job training before they work independently](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/sales/sales-engineers.htm), and that they typically need a bachelor's degree in engineering or a related field. For technical writers, BLS says [knowledge of a technical subject such as science or engineering is beneficial](https://www.bls.gov/ooh/media-and-communication/technical-writers.htm).
:::

BLS profiles describe US occupations as a whole. They are context, not a description of any one employer's entry-level role.

## How postings describe them

The same work appears under many titles, so search for several of them:

- **QA:** QA engineer, test engineer, SDET, software engineer in test, validation engineer.
- **Pre-sales:** solutions engineer, sales engineer, solutions consultant, technical account associate.
- **Support:** technical support engineer, support analyst, help desk, service desk, application support.
- **Implementation:** implementation specialist, implementation consultant, onboarding specialist, customer success associate.
- **Data:** data analyst, business analyst, reporting analyst, operations analyst.
- **Engineering-side:** quality engineer, manufacturing engineer, process engineer, field engineer, field service engineer, test engineer.
- **Writing:** technical writer, documentation specialist, content developer.

Read the duties. "Support engineer" can mean a phone queue at one company and debugging production code at another. [Reading a new grad posting](../reading-a-new-grad-posting/) covers level markers and experience floors.

## How to judge one as a first role

An adjacent role is a good first job when it builds skills you can show later. Ask these questions in the recruiter screen, or answer them from the posting:

1. **What is the daily work?** Ask for a typical week. How much time goes on building, analysing or writing, and how much on queues or travel?
2. **What tools will I use?** Code, test frameworks, SQL, CAD, statistical tools, or mainly ticketing and email?
3. **Who do I work with?** Roles that sit with engineering, product or operations teams give you a view of their work.
4. **How is performance measured?** Ticket counts, quota, defect rates, project delivery?
5. **Where have people in this role moved next?** Ask for examples rather than a promise.
6. **What are the hours and travel?** Support can include shifts or on-call. Field roles can involve extended travel. Sales roles can include variable pay; see [Understanding new grad compensation](../understanding-compensation/).

If the answers show you will learn the tools and the domain, the role can be a sound start in its own right, not only a step to something else. Do not take a role only to transfer out of it, and do not assume an internal move is possible unless the employer tells you so.

## Use your existing evidence

Most adjacent roles want the evidence you already have, framed for their work:

- A class project with a test suite fits QA.
- A capstone presentation or a teaching-assistant role fits pre-sales and implementation.
- A lab report or senior thesis fits technical writing.
- A data cleaning and analysis project fits analyst roles.

[Tailoring your résumé](../tailoring-your-resume/) explains how to reorder the same experience for a different posting without inventing anything. If you are coming from a non-CS major, read [Career switchers, other majors and bootcamp graduates](../career-switchers-and-non-cs-majors/).

## Find them on the board

The board files each posting under one ROLE category, chosen mainly from title words. Adjacent roles are spread across several categories:

- **support**: technical support, help desk, service desk, customer success, implementation and onboarding specialists.
- **engineering**: quality, manufacturing, process, test, field and validation engineers.
- **infra**: systems administrator titles.
- **data analyst** and **business analyst**: analyst titles.
- **swe**: solutions engineers and most QA and SDET titles (test-automation and test engineer titles usually go to engineering).
- **sales**: titles that contain "sales", including sales engineer.
- **consulting** and **arts & media**: an "implementation consultant" can be filed under consulting, and a technical writer under arts & media.

If a role is missing from the chip you expected, leave ROLE empty and type the title into the search box, for example `QA`, `solutions engineer` or `technical writer`. [How NGJ classifies jobs](../how-ngj-classifies-jobs/) explains the rules. You can also browse every category from the [browse hub](../../jobs/).

## A one-page comparison

For each adjacent role you are considering, note the title, the posting's top three duties, the tools named, the team it works with, the hours or travel, the pay structure, and one piece of your own evidence that fits. Then rank the roles against your [target-company list](../build-a-target-company-list/), not against the title you started with.
