A target list is what turns the scraper's thousands of rows into a few hundred worth opening. Build it once, refresh it monthly, and let it decide where your tailoring effort goes.

## Size

Aim for 30 to 60 companies. Fewer and you run out of options when one of them stops hiring; many more and you cannot track any of them. Split the list into three tiers:

- **A — would accept tomorrow.** Ten to twenty companies. Track hiring managers, read engineering blogs, follow on LinkedIn.
- **B — would apply to a strong role.** Another fifteen to twenty. Track loosely.
- **C — long shots.** The rest, kept so you do not miss the surprise posting from a household name that opens a new grad program.

## Pick by what is real

A target list built from vibes falls apart. Anchor each name to criteria you can verify:

- **Hiring volume.** The board has a [page per company](../../jobs/). A company with zero roles this quarter is not a target.
- **New grad track.** Look for postings with the words "new grad", "entry level", "associate", or " Rotational " in the title. See [What counts as a new grad job](../what-counts-as-a-new-grad-job/).
- **Sponsorship.** If you need it, the [visa flags guide](../visa-sponsorship-and-citizenship/) explains which chips to keep on.
- **Location and remote.** Decide where you will physically work and never apply outside it. Remote-only postings are their own category.
- **Compensation.** [Understanding compensation](../understanding-compensation/) explains what the board shows and what it does not.
- **Industry.** You will be here for two to four years; pick one you can talk about in an interview.

## What to do with the list

Use it as the top filter when you scan the board's daily new roles. Anything at an A or B company gets your full tailoring; anything at a C company gets your base résumé and a quick check. Refer to [Application strategy](../application-strategy/) for the tiers and pace.

Refresh the list monthly. The board's [new this week](../../jobs/new-this-week/) page will show you which A and B companies are actively hiring right now, which is more useful than last quarter's memory.
