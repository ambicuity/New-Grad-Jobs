Finishing a degree without a job lined up is not a verdict on your chances. What changes after graduation is not your value but your situation: campus deadlines have passed, you have more hours in the day, and some programs have eligibility windows that start counting from your graduation date. This guide covers keeping a steady routine, talking about the time since graduation, finding roles that start soon, and checking whether "new grad" postings still apply to you.

## Keep a routine

Without classes, the search can expand to fill every hour or shrink to nothing. A fixed weekly shape helps more than a daily target:

- **Two or three application blocks a week.** Check new postings, apply to the ones that match your target list, and record each one.
- **One preparation block.** Interview practice, a skill gap from recent postings, or one finished project.
- **One outreach block.** A few messages to people at target employers, former instructors or classmates. [Networking as a new grad](../networking-as-a-new-grad/) has the wording.
- **One review block.** Look at where applications stall using [Diagnose your job-search funnel](../diagnose-your-job-search-funnel/) and change one thing at a time.

Keep the rest of the week for something that is not the search: work, study, volunteering or rest. A part-time job while you search is not a setback, and it can be evidence on your résumé.

Check whether your university's career office still serves recent graduates. Some offer alumni appointments, job boards or résumé reviews for a period after graduation; ask yours what is available.

## Talk about the gap honestly

Time between graduation and a first job needs a sentence, not a defense. Say what you have been doing, briefly and truthfully, then return to why you fit the role. Adapt one of these:

> I finished my degree in May and have been searching full time since then. I've also been building a dashboard for a local food bank, which is where most of my recent SQL work comes from.

> After graduating I worked part time at a pharmacy while applying for lab roles. That job is also where I got used to documentation and double-checking work under time pressure.

> I took a few months after my degree for a family matter. That's resolved, and I'm available to start now.

You do not have to share private reasons, and you should not invent a job, a project or a date to cover the time. If the time included a course, a certificate, a part-time job or a project, list it on the résumé with real dates.

## Look for roles that start soon

After graduation, one advantage you have is availability. Some employers hire on a rolling basis and want someone who can start within weeks, while others hire a cohort with a fixed start date. To find the first kind:

- **Check the board often.** Rolling-hire roles can be posted at any time of year, and recent ones appear on [new this week](../../jobs/new-this-week/).
- **Read the posting for start dates.** "Immediate start", "start date: ASAP" or a start date in the next few months are signals; a cohort start a year away is a different timeline.
- **Say you are available.** Put your availability in the application or the first recruiter conversation: "I've graduated and can start within two weeks."
- **Widen what you will consider.** [Adjacent technical roles](../adjacent-technical-roles/) and roles in a nearby field can be a realistic first step.

If you are on F-1 OPT, the clock on unemployment days matters more than the calendar. Read [OPT unemployment days](../opt-unemployment-days/) and confirm your count with your DSO.

## Use WIDEN SCOPE on the board

The board's curated list has fixed rules, and one of its soft rules, the 60-day window, can hide postings that suit you now. The WIDEN SCOPE toggles under the filters show near misses that failed only soft rules:

- **posted 60–120 days ago** shows still-listed postings older than the curated 60-day window. Some of these will be closed or filled; open the employer's page to check before you apply. [Expired jobs](../expired-jobs/) explains the closed flag.
- **level III+** and the other toggles are less likely to fit, but a near miss appears only when you turn on every reason it failed.

These rows are tagged with the reason and never counted in the board's numbers. If you think the rules miss whole kinds of titles, the EXPLORE tab lets you search every posting the last run saw with your own words. [Finding hidden new grad roles](../finding-hidden-new-grad-roles/) and [How NGJ classifies jobs](../how-ngj-classifies-jobs/) explain both.

## Check whether you still fit "new grad" postings

There is no single rule for how long you count as a new graduate. Each employer or program sets its own window, if it sets one at all, and many postings set none. Read the eligibility section of each posting for phrases such as a graduation date range, "recent graduate", or "within N months of graduation", and ask the recruiter when it is unclear.

::: evidence official
The US federal government's Pathways Recent Graduates Program is one example of a written window: [OPM's Pathways page](https://www.opm.gov/policy-data-oversight/hiring-information/students-recent-graduates/) says applicants must apply within two years of degree or certificate completion, with up to six years for veterans whose military service obligation prevented applying sooner.
:::

That window applies to that program only. Other employers may use a different window, a graduation-year range, or none. Do not assume you are out because of an earlier date, and do not assume you are in because one program accepted it.

Some "entry level" or "Associate" postings set no graduation window at all, because they are about experience, not graduation date. Those stay open to you as long as your experience fits; [What counts as a new grad job?](../what-counts-as-a-new-grad-job/) describes the signals the board reads.

## A checklist for this month

1. Write down your weekly routine and put the blocks in a calendar.
2. Draft and rehearse your two-sentence gap answer.
3. Add any part-time work, course or project since graduation to your résumé with real dates.
4. Turn on WIDEN SCOPE "posted 60–120 days ago" once a week and check each lead on the employer's site.
5. Note each target program's eligibility window, as the program itself states it.
6. Ask your career office what it offers recent alumni.
