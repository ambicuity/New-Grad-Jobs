Recruiters triage dozens of inboxes a day. The message that gets read is short, specific, and tells them which role and which person you are.

## Who to message

Recruiters at companies on your [target-company list](../build-a-target-company-list/), ideally the one who posted or is hiring for the role you want. You can find them by:

- Searching LinkedIn for `recruiter` plus the company name.
- Looking at the bottom of a job posting; many name the recruiter.
- Asking a current employee to introduce you by name.

Prioritize the recruiter responsible for the role, whether in-house or an authorized agency. Verify their identity and the vacancy.

## What to write

Four lines. No greeting paragraph, no "I came across your profile", no résumé attachment.

> Hi [name], I'm a [program] grad applying to the [role title] at [company] (link). My background is in [one sentence of specifics: stack, project, or relevant internship]. I've applied through the careers portal — would you be the right person to flag it to, or is there another contact for this opening?

The link to the role is essential; do not make the recruiter look it up. The "right person" question is the move that turns a cold message into a useful one, because the recruiter either confirms or redirects.

## What not to do

- Avoid unsolicited attachments in a first message; offer to send a résumé through the recruiter's preferred channel.
- No follow-up before five business days have passed.
- No second message that is longer than the first.
- No asking the recruiter for a referral. They are the channel; they are not the referrer.

## When they reply

Reply with the same brevity. If they ask for a résumé, send the requested file or format through a verified channel. Offer availability for a call; if their proposed slot does not work, suggest alternatives promptly.

If you do not hear back, you cannot infer the role's status or your fit from silence. Apply through the portal and move to the next company on your list.
