Recruiters triage dozens of inboxes a day. The message that gets read is short, specific, and tells them which role and which person you are.

## Who to message

Recruiters at companies on your [target-company list](../build-a-target-company-list/), ideally the one who posted or is hiring for the role you want. You can find them by:

- Searching LinkedIn for `recruiter` plus the company name.
- Looking at the bottom of a job posting; many name the recruiter.
- Asking a current employee to introduce you by name.

Skip third-party agency recruiters unless the role is sourced through them; in-house recruiters are the ones who can move your application.

## What to write

Four lines. No greeting paragraph, no "I came across your profile", no résumé attachment.

> Hi [name], I'm a [program] grad applying to the [role title] at [company] (link). My background is in [one sentence of specifics: stack, project, or relevant internship]. I've applied through the careers portal — would you be the right person to flag it to, or should I apply for someone specific on the team?

The link to the role is essential; do not make the recruiter look it up. The "right person" question is the move that turns a cold message into a useful one, because the recruiter either confirms or redirects.

## What not to do

- No attachments. Recruiters cannot open them and your message gets archived.
- No follow-up before five business days have passed.
- No second message that is longer than the first.
- No asking the recruiter for a referral. They are the channel; they are not the referrer.

## When they reply

Reply with the same brevity. If they ask for a résumé, send the link to your LinkedIn profile, not the PDF. If they ask to set up a call, take the slot they offer; rescheduling costs you.

If you do not hear back, the role is probably not the one to push on. Apply through the portal and move to the next company on your list.
