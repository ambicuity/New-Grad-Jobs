This guide is for graduates who need US work authorization beyond OPT, or who are not on F-1 at all, and want to know which routes exist before they talk to an employer. It covers two things: H-1B employers that are not subject to the annual cap, and four other classifications that some graduates qualify for because of their citizenship or record. Every route here needs an employer, and most involve the employer's immigration attorney. Use this to learn the vocabulary and the questions, then confirm with the employer's immigration team or a qualified immigration attorney. The basics of OPT and the H-1B cap season are in [OPT and STEM OPT](../opt-and-stem-opt/) and the [international student job search center](../international-student-job-search-center/).

## What "cap-exempt" means

USCIS's [H-1B specialty occupations page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/h-1b-specialty-occupations) states that the H-1B classification "has an annual numerical limit (cap) of 65,000 new statuses or visas each fiscal year," with an additional 20,000 petitions for beneficiaries with a master's degree or higher from a US institution of higher education. The same page says H-1B workers "who are petitioned for or employed at an institution of higher education" or its related or affiliated nonprofit entities, "a nonprofit research organization, or a government research organization" are not subject to the cap. The employer, or its agent, files the petition, Form I-129.

For a new graduate, the practical difference is timing. A cap-subject petition goes through the registration and selection season described on the [H-1B cap season page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/h-1b-specialty-occupations/h-1b-cap-season). The regulation ties registration to cap-subject petitions, so a cap-exempt petition does not wait for that selection. That does not mean a cap-exempt employer will sponsor you, or that a particular petition will be approved.

## The cap-exempt categories in the regulation

::: evidence official
[8 CFR 214.2(h)(8)(iii)(F)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-8/chapter-I/subchapter-B/part-214/subpart-A/section-214.2) defines an "institution of higher education" by reference to the Higher Education Act, and treats a nonprofit as related to or affiliated with one if it shares ownership or control with it, is operated by it, is attached as a member, branch, cooperative or subsidiary, or has a formal written affiliation agreement and a fundamental activity of directly contributing to the institution's research or education mission.
:::

The same regulation defines the research categories by what the organization does. A nonprofit is a nonprofit research organization "when a fundamental activity" is basic or applied research. A governmental organization is a government research organization when a fundamental activity is performing or promoting basic or applied research, and it "may be a Federal, State, or local entity."

Three further rules in that paragraph matter for job seekers:

- **Working at a qualifying employer you are not employed by.** You can still qualify if you spend at least half your work time doing duties at the qualifying institution, and those duties directly further its higher education, nonprofit research or government research mission. USCIS says it "will focus on the job duties to be performed, rather than where the duties are physically performed."
- **Leaving cap-exempt work.** If the cap-exempt employment ends and you have no new cap-exempt petition, you become subject to the cap unless you were previously counted against it within that six-year period of H-1B admission.
- **A second job.** Concurrent H-1B employment in a cap-subject position does not subject you to the cap while the cap-exempt job continues, according to the regulation. If the cap-exempt employment ends, you become subject to the cap unless you were previously counted against it within that six-year period.

Whether a specific employer qualifies is a legal question about that employer. A university hospital, a research institute or a city agency may or may not meet the definitions. Ask the employer's immigration team, not a forum.

USCIS's [H-1B page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/h-1b-specialty-occupations) also describes a $100,000 payment for certain new petitions under a September 2025 Proclamation. USCIS says a federal court vacated its guidance implementing that payment and that DHS is complying while it appeals, but plans to collect the payment if the order is lifted. Check the page for the current status and ask the employer's attorney whether it applies to you.

## Other routes, as USCIS describes them

USCIS's [temporary (nonimmigrant) workers page](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-nonimmigrant-workers) says "your prospective employer must generally file a nonimmigrant petition on your behalf with USCIS," and notes that E-3 and TN are among the classifications where some workers can work without an employer first filing a petition. Even then, you need a qualifying US job offer.

| Route | Who USCIS says it is for | What the employer provides |
| --- | --- | --- |
| [TN](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/tn-usmca-professionals) | Citizens of Canada or Mexico whose profession is included in the USMCA, in a position that requires a USMCA professional, with a prearranged full-time or part-time job and the qualifications to practise | A job offer and letter; or a Form I-129 petition |
| [E-3](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/e-3-specialty-occupation-workers-from-australia) | Nationals of Australia coming to perform services in a specialty occupation | A Labor Condition Application filed for E-3 and a job offer at the higher of the actual or prevailing wage |
| H-1B1 | "Free Trade Agreement workers in a specialty occupation from Chile and Singapore" | A job offer in a specialty occupation; [Form I-129](https://www.uscis.gov/i-129) for changes or extensions in the US |
| [O-1](https://www.uscis.gov/working-in-the-united-states/temporary-workers/o-1-visa-individuals-with-extraordinary-ability-or-achievement) | People with extraordinary ability in the sciences, education, business or athletics (O-1A) or the arts, motion pictures or television (O-1B), shown by sustained national or international acclaim (or, for film and television, a record of extraordinary achievement) | A petition by a US employer or agent, with a written advisory opinion from a peer group or expert |

A few details from those pages:

- **TN for Canadians.** USCIS says Canadian citizens residing outside the US do not need a TN visa and can establish eligibility at a port of entry or pre-clearance station with proof of citizenship and an employer letter. Mexican citizens residing outside the US need a TN visa from a US embassy or consulate. If you are already in the US, for example on F-1, ask the employer's attorney about a change of status on Form I-129. The initial admission is up to three years.
- **E-3.** USCIS requires a bachelor's or higher degree in the specific specialty, or its equivalent, and any required licence before you start work.
- **H-1B1.** The cap season page says up to 6,800 visas are set aside from the 65,000 regular cap for the H-1B1 programme. Ask the employer's attorney how they would file.
- **O-1.** This is an evidence-heavy classification. Treat it as a conversation with an attorney, not a default route for a new graduate.

This guide gives no processing times or approval odds. Those change, and the employer's attorney can tell you what USCIS currently publishes for your case type.

## Questions to ask an employer

1. Do you consider yourselves cap-exempt for this role? Who confirmed that?
2. If I am a Canadian, Mexican, Australian, Chilean or Singaporean citizen, have you hired people on TN, E-3 or H-1B1 before, and who handles it?
3. Who is your immigration counsel, and will they talk to me before I accept?
4. If this role ends, what would that mean for my status? Ask the attorney, not the recruiter.
