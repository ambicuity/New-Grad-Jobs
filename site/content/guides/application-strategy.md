Applying is a numbers problem and a quality problem at once. Too few applications and variance decides the outcome; generic applications can make relevant evidence harder to see. The strategy is to split applications into tiers and spend effort where it changes the result.

## Three tiers

- **Targets**: roles and companies you would accept tomorrow. Tailor the résumé, write a short cover note if the form has one, and look for a referral. A handful a week. See [How to build a target-company list](../build-a-target-company-list/).
- **Good fits**: roles that match your profile at companies you know little about. Base résumé, quick review of the posting, apply. Most of your applications.
- **Long shots**: roles slightly above your profile or at very selective employers. Base résumé, no extra effort, no expectations. Apply when they appear; the cost is minutes.

The board's saved list (`s`) is a natural inbox for targets and good fits. Star during the daily skim, apply in a sitting.

## Speed matters

Many employers review on a rolling basis and cohort roles close when the class fills. Apply promptly when the role fits, without sacrificing eligibility checks or application quality. The board's NEW 24H marker uses the source's posted timestamp; feeds update after successful publication and your reader's refresh, and the [new this week](../../jobs/new-this-week/) page shows what is being added.

## Track everything

Keep one tracker with a row per application: company, title, link to the job page, date, résumé version, status, contact, next step. A spreadsheet is enough. On the board, press `a` to mark a job applied so it stops looking new; the LINK button copies a stable page for the job that you can paste into the tracker. The EXPLORE tab's EXPORT CSV button gives you a spreadsheet-ready list if you prefer to start from data.

Review the tracker weekly. Keep pending applications separate from rejections. Follow the employer's update schedule and keep applying; a month of silence does not establish that a role is closed.

## Referrals

A referral can help an employer understand your relevant work, but review order and eligibility depend on the company. Ask alumni, former internship colleagues and people you have actually talked to, and make it easy: send the job link and a two-line summary of why you fit. For a cold contact, explain the connection and give the person an easy way to decline rather than expecting an immediate endorsement. Read [How to ask for a referral](../how-to-ask-for-a-referral/) for the full playbook.

## When nothing comes back

Diagnose before changing everything:

- **No responses at all across many good fits**: check eligibility, targeting, résumé clarity, employer timelines and market conditions. Test it against [An ATS-friendly résumé](../ats-friendly-resume/) and have two people read it for ten seconds and tell you what they remember. If the funnel is silent past a hundred applications, read [No interviews after 100 applications](../no-interviews-after-100-applications/).
- **Responses but no interviews past the recruiter screen**: review your explanation of fit and the logistics discussed. Practice the two-minute summary of who you are and what you want.
- **Interviews but no offers**: look for concrete skill or communication gaps, and ask whether headcount or another candidate affected the decision. See [The interview process](../interview-process/) and [Getting interviews but no offers](../getting-interviews-but-no-offers/) for which round is breaking.

For a fuller view of where the funnel is leaking, read [How to diagnose your job-search funnel](../diagnose-your-job-search-funnel/). And after each interview, fill in a postmortem so the next attempt is informed by the last one — see [How to learn from every interview](../interview-postmortem/).

## Pace

A search takes months, and its intensity should vary with the season: heavy when your target employers post, lighter when they do not. [The new grad hiring timeline](../new-grad-hiring-timeline/) explains the calendar. A steady pace with rest beats bursts followed by weeks of nothing.
