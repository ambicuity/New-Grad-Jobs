# Guide review — September 30, 2026

PR [#374](https://github.com/ambicuity/New-Grad-Jobs/pull/374) was open when
reviewed. Its pre-commit check failed because twelve new Markdown files lacked
final newlines. The other CI checks on the original head passed.

The review covered the 76-guide collection's metadata, supported markup,
guide links and rendering, with detailed factual corrections to the twelve
new guides and consistency corrections to related existing guides.

## Corrections

- Removed guarantees about referral review, portfolio requirements, negotiation
  outcomes and response rates. Distinguished advice from measured findings.
- Separated fixed cash, target bonuses, equity, benefits and one-time payments;
  removed the assumption that an occupation-wide median is a new-grad pay target.
- Replaced repeated-prompt AI fact checking with verification against records
  and primary sources. Added privacy and assessment-policy guidance.
- Revised US student guidance for the September 2026 admission-rule transition,
  filing conditions, combined unemployment limit and FY 2027 H-1B selection.
  Individual transition eligibility requires DSO or immigration-counsel review.
- Grounded accommodation guidance in employer coverage and jurisdiction;
  distinguished language preferences from disability accommodation rights.
- Made scam verification account for authorized agencies and third-party ATS
  domains. Added independent verification and recovery/reporting sources.
- Corrected NEW 24H versus first-seen descriptions, sponsorship-flag matching,
  missing company-directory links, and unsupported single-asterisk emphasis.
- Fixed table parsing that dropped the next line, double-escaped query parameters
  in source links, and a content-test condition that missed local anchor errors.

## Maintenance

The review date is a snapshot, not a certification that policies will remain
unchanged. Recheck market releases when published and immigration, accommodation,
pay-disclosure and assessment rules before relying on them. Preserve reference
periods and populations; do not turn board counts into national hiring estimates.

Do not change a guide's updated date without reviewing its affected claims.
Use explicit examples for worksheets and keep recommendations separate from
empirical claims. Official or primary evidence callouts should link directly
to the supporting source.
